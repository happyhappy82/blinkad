Work Through the Profile Basics

  • Accurate details: check the real-world name, address or appropriate service-area arrangement, phone, website where available, hours and accurate categories. “100% complete” is not a universal eligibility score; some fields are optional.
  • Useful photos: show the actual exterior, interior and representative products or services with appropriate permission. The original ten-photo suggestion is a starter collection, not a Google ranking minimum.
  • Reviews: offer genuine customers an optional, neutral way to give feedback. Do not limit requests to satisfied customers or connect a review event, gift or discount to posting. Replies should address the actual concern and describe only facts and action you can support.
  • Posts: share relevant menu changes, events, offers or closure notices. Check dates, conditions and Google’s publication status; a routine does not reserve a search position.
  • Location language: use Godeok 1-dong, Gangdong-gu or the real building naturally where helpful. Repeating a place name or an unsupported “best” claim does not make the service explanation more useful.

Choose Work You Can Keep Up With

  • Local details: help visitors recognize the street, entrance and actual service. Avoid claims such as “the tastiest café” or “the only academy” without a basis.
  • Competitor context: review relevant public profiles to understand unanswered questions and presentation, without copying content or treating the source’s 52 agencies and 50 education businesses as current measured search competition.
  • Other platforms: keep Naver Place and other relevant channels accurate where your customers use them. Different platforms have their own features and rules; benefits should be measured rather than assumed to transfer.
  • Community communication: share useful information only where local-community rules permit it, making the business connection clear. A community link is not a guarantee of word-of-mouth or ranking.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does registering a Business Profile cost money?

Google provides the profile service without a subscription charge for eligible businesses. An agency’s setup or management fee and paid advertising are separate services that should be explained clearly.

How can I make it easier for customers to leave reviews?

A review link or QR code can provide a convenient route for genuine customers. Keep participation optional, allow any honest opinion and avoid incentives, positive-only requests, pressure or requested wording. The source’s review-event example should not become a reward for posting.

Should I keep updating the profile?

Update important facts when they change and use a manageable check to catch omissions. Add worthwhile photos or news when relevant. Do not edit accurate details or create unnecessary posts simply to appear active.
