“How much should we set aside each month for Google Ads?” It is a natural starting question. Yet two restaurants or clinics can need different budgets depending on the location, language, keywords and action they want a customer to take.

At BlinkAd, we recommend starting with estimated click costs and a way to identify actual inquiries. Understanding how costs arise also makes agency proposals easier to compare.

How Google Ads costs work

Distinguish payment methods from bidding strategies. In Google’s Target CPA guidance, the CPA target is a desired average cost per conversion used to guide bidding. Choosing Target CPA does not mean every campaign charges only when an inquiry occurs.

Term How to read it
CPC Cost per click: the amount spent to obtain clicks
CPM Cost per thousand impressions
CPA or cost per conversion Cost per action defined as a conversion; check what action is counted
Target CPA A strategy that bids toward an average conversion-cost target; check the payment method separately

Google separately describes paying for conversions for eligible Display campaigns. Check the actual campaign settings rather than combining Search and Display payment explanations.

How does a daily budget relate to monthly spending?

Google’s budget overview explains that, for most campaigns, the daily spending limit is twice the average daily budget and the monthly charging limit is 30.4 times that budget. If an average daily budget stays at KRW 10,000 throughout the month, the corresponding monthly limit is KRW 304,000. Check campaign-specific rules and the effects of budget changes too.

Read the budget together with the charging limits rather than expecting identical spending every day.

What to check in industry CPC figures

When a table gives an “average restaurant CPC” or “average clinic CPC,” look for the period, country, language, keywords and ad type behind it. An industry label alone is insufficient for pricing your campaign.

Use Google Keyword Planner to explore the keywords and targeting relevant to the campaign. Keep forecasts separate from actual operating costs. For international customers, consider the search language as well as the geographic area.

A monthly budget calculation example

To illustrate the calculation, assume a CPC of KRW 600 and an inquiry conversion rate of 3%. These are inputs for an example, not an industry average or a measured BlinkAd client result. Replace them with relevant estimates and actual data when planning your budget.

Assumed ad spend Estimated clicks Assumed inquiries
KRW 300,000 About 500 About 15
KRW 500,000 About 833 About 25
KRW 1,000,000 About 1,667 About 50

The calculations are spend divided by CPC, then clicks multiplied by the inquiry conversion rate. Agency fees and landing-page production are excluded. Track inquiries, confirmed bookings and payments separately.

Set an affordable test budget and a goal, then examine the search terms receiving spend and the inquiries actually received. The appropriate budget and observation period depend on search demand and the evidence collected.

Comparing Google Ads and SEO costs

Pausing advertising stops paid advertising exposure. Being found through organic search requires separate website and content work. SEO still involves production, updates and maintenance, and a particular ranking or timetable cannot be assumed. Google explains that meeting its Search requirements does not ensure inclusion.

Cost or result Advertising SEO and content
Direct work or spending Media ad spend Content and website improvements
Ongoing work Account, creative and landing-page checks Information updates and technical and editorial checks
Results to examine Spend, clicks, inquiries and qualified conversations Search exposure, visits, inquiries and qualified conversations

Start by distinguishing time-sensitive promotion from information customers need over time. Both forms of work may reveal gaps on the same landing page.

Three checks before a small business starts advertising

1. Match searches to the service you actually provide

Define the area, business category and service, then examine the page customers reach. Making a keyword longer does not automatically improve its cost or conversion performance. Adjust using actual search terms and results.

2. Separate button clicks from accepted inquiries

A click on a phone or booking button is different from a completed conversation or booking. Agree with the agency on the conversion definition and compare it with the inquiry records.

3. Use Quality Score for diagnosis

Google describes Quality Score as a diagnostic tool and states that the score itself is not an input in the ad auction. Examine relevance, the destination page and the inquiry process alongside the diagnostic score.

Prepare to be found beyond paid advertising

Keep the Business Profile’s address, opening hours, menu and photographs accurate for customers who need that information. Google identifies relevance, distance and prominence as main local-ranking factors. Avoid treating a fixed number of photos or posts as a promised ranking improvement.

Frequently asked questions

What is a sensible starting budget?

Start with the estimated costs of the target keywords, available funds and the inquiries you want to measure. Ask why a proposed amount is appropriate and what costs it includes beyond media spend.

Should we prioritize Google Ads or Naver advertising?

Compare the customer’s language, service and destination page. Use observed visits and inquiries to adjust the allocation rather than choosing by age alone.

Are agency fees separate from ad spend?

Check the proposal for media spend, management fees, creative and page production, tracking setup and tax. Compare the actual scope rather than assuming one commission rate applies to every provider.
