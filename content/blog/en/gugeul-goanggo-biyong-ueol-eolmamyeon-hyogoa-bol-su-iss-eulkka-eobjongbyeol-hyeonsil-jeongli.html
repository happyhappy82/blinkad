“How much would we need to spend to start advertising on Google?”

It is a common question from business owners. You may have a rough idea of Naver advertising costs but find Google's pricing less familiar. We will explain the main cost measures, work through the industry ranges used in our original article, and show what a monthly budget might buy under stated assumptions.

How Google Ads costs work

Three terms are useful, although they do not all describe the event that triggers a charge:

MeasureMeaningTypical context
CPC: cost per clickA charge for a click on an adSearch and Shopping ads
CPM: cost per thousand impressionsA cost measure based on 1,000 impressions; billing may use viewable impressionsDisplay and some video advertising
CPA: cost per actionCost divided by recorded conversions; a target CPA is a bidding goal, not a general promise to charge only when a conversion happensConversion-focused bidding; eligible Display campaigns have a separate pay-for-conversions option

For a business considering CPC-based Search ads, clicks are the starting cost measure. You set an average daily budget, then monitor actual spend and results. It is an average rather than a hard daily cap: for most campaigns, Google's daily spending limit is twice that average, with a monthly limit of 30.4 times the average daily budget. A fixed KRW 10,000 daily budget therefore corresponds to about KRW 300,000 a month—KRW 304,000 under the usual monthly-limit calculation.

You can start with a small budget; the original article used KRW 1,000 a day as an illustration. That is not a universal minimum or a promise of enough delivery to evaluate a campaign. The useful starting amount depends on the keywords, location and objective.

Advertising can help you test demand sooner. SEO can build information that remains useful over time, with ongoing maintenance. Neither is automatically cheaper per customer. Compare the total work and cost with the inquiries, sales or visits you can reasonably attribute to it; the two approaches can also work together.

Three things to watch when starting

1. Choose keywords with a clear connection to your business

A broad phrase such as “good restaurants” may attract people with very different plans. A more specific phrase such as “lunch near Gangnam Station” gives you a clearer location and occasion to work with. Start with relevant searches and review the actual search terms; a longer phrase is not automatically cheaper or more profitable.

2. Set up conversion tracking

Track meaningful actions such as calls, directions requests and booking steps, while labeling exactly what each action measures. A booking-button click is not a confirmed reservation or a sale. Tracking helps identify useful traffic; linking it to actual business results takes an appropriate follow-up process.

3. Use Quality Score to find areas to improve

For Search campaigns, Quality Score is a diagnostic based on expected clickthrough rate, ad relevance and landing-page experience. Google explicitly says the displayed score is not an auction input. Use its components to investigate problems rather than treating a higher score as a guaranteed position or discount.

Being found without paying for every click

In conversations with owners, we often hear concerns about a recurring advertising bill. Local SEO and a well-maintained Google Business Profile are worth considering alongside paid campaigns.

For a local search such as “Gangnam café” or “Hongdae hair salon,” Google may show a map-based group of business results. Appearing there can create opportunities for visits without paying for those organic clicks; its position on the results page and your inclusion are not fixed.

If you have not created a profile, our Google Business Profile registration guide (Korean) explains the starting process. Keep the information, photographs and genuine review responses useful and current. Keep doing that work to help customers assess the business; regular posting alone does not guarantee a higher ranking.

Frequently asked questions

What is the smallest budget I can start with?

The original article illustrated a very small start at KRW 1,000 a day and suggested around KRW 300,000 a month for an initial test. Choose the amount for your campaign and expected click costs, rather than treating either number as a platform-wide threshold or an assurance of results.

Will we disappear from Google if we pause the ads?

Pausing a campaign stops its ads from serving. It does not, by itself, remove your website's organic listing or Business Profile. Organic visibility has its own conditions and can change; it is not guaranteed to remain indefinitely without maintenance.

Should we invest in Naver or Google first?

Start with how your intended customers search. Google may be relevant to international visitors, while Naver may matter for other audiences. Age alone is not a reliable rule for allocating the budget. If resources permit, run comparable, carefully measured tests and use the results to decide.

Does using an agency add a fee?

Agency management can be charged separately from media spend. The original article mentioned 10–20% of spend or a fixed monthly fee as possible models; that range was not a verified market survey or a BlinkAd quotation. Ask for the actual scope, fees, taxes and media-spend treatment in writing. A simpler self-managed campaign may suit some small tests.

Start with a test you can evaluate

Google Ads costs vary with the industry, query and competition. Begin with a manageable test, define the results you need to observe, and adjust using the evidence.

For longer-term discovery, consider useful website content and a complete Business Profile alongside advertising. They can continue helping customers after a particular campaign ends, provided the information remains relevant and accurate.

If recurring ad spend is a concern, reviewing those basics is a practical first step.
