Give Customers Information They Can Use

Google Business Profile is free for eligible businesses that meet customers in person. Authorized owners can maintain information associated with Search and Maps. Local results depend mainly on relevance, distance and prominence; a profile does not reserve first position.

The source’s high local-business share does not establish a preference for independent shops. Its examples included Yes Victoria, a cleaning-related business on the eighth floor, and Urban 245, a clothing-related business on the second floor. These are historical references, not independently verified current clients, addresses or services. Explain the actual entrance and contact arrangements rather than inferring visibility or foot traffic from a floor.

Prepare the Details Customers Will Use

1. Start with What Someone Wants to Know

  • Choose real service topics: Korean food, a meat restaurant, hair, nails, property advice, a study café or office rental in Naseong-dong can suggest different questions. These are English planning examples, not newly measured Korean queries or a reason to claim services the business does not provide.
  • Explain the decision: restaurant customers may need a menu, prices, setting, parking and hours; salon customers may need actual services, stylist information, permitted work examples and a booking route. A query does not establish an immediate purchase.

2. Keep the Everyday Details Accurate

  • Name: use the recognized real-world business name, without added location or service terms solely for SEO.
  • Address: provide the actual eligible location or appropriate service area. Hannuri-daero, Naseong-ro and Naseong Buk-ro are source road examples. Write Sejong City once in the source’s illustrative 96 Naseong-ro address and confirm the actual premises before using it.
  • Hours: maintain regular hours and applicable special-hours fields for holidays or changes, then check the public result.
  • Phone: provide a working number that reaches the business.
  • Categories: choose an accurate available primary category and only relevant additional ones. A meat dish or service can be explained in suitable content; it is not automatically an available category.
  • Website: link to a relevant, working page with useful service and contact information.
  • Services and menus: explain what is actually offered, including accurate prices and conditions where appropriate. Keep those details current.
  • Photos: show the real entrance, space, products or service, with suitable permissions for people and customer material.

3. Share Real Changes and Respond to Actual Feedback

  • Posts: use supported features for actual news, events, menus or offers with current dates and terms. Check publication status.
  • Reviews: invite genuine customers neutrally and leave feedback optional, without incentives, positive-only selection, pressure or requested wording. Reply to specific facts and protect private information, describing supported actions rather than promising a better future visit.
  • Common questions: answer recurring issues through the website or currently supported profile features. The former Q&A API ended on November 3, 2025; that does not establish a uniform question feature on every profile or the disappearance of all such interfaces.
  • Current visuals: replace misleading images when the setting or offering changes. Customer photos are not automatically available for reuse in promotional content; check permissions.

4. Make the Actual Location Clear

  • Use the correct building: The Central, Naritjae Village Complex 3 and Di Felice were source examples, not verified client addresses. Mention a building and floor only when they genuinely help someone find the business; the illustrative first-floor example needs confirmation before use.
  • Explain the entrance: the source listed ground-floor businesses at 21.0%, floors two to five at 29.8%, and the sixth floor or above at 8.6%. These partial figures do not prove accessibility. Show actual stairs, lifts, signage and approach details accurately.
  • Describe real service differences: a menu, group seating or children’s area can be useful information if it actually exists. Do not infer amenities, exclusivity or customer demand from the source’s category counts.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a profile essential for every Naseong-dong business?

Check eligibility and how customers find the business. An eligible business can use a free profile to maintain useful Search and Maps information. The source’s counts and local-business share do not establish residents’ search behaviour or make Google every business’s most effective channel. Choose the wider scope from actual customer needs.

How should review replies be handled?

Read the specific feedback, check relevant facts and reply where you can answer a concern or add a useful update. Keep personal information private and apologize appropriately when the facts support it. Avoid repeated templates or promises about a future visit. A quick reply does not guarantee trust, a changed review or new customers.

Which information should be updated first?

Correct changed hours, prices, availability and contact routes promptly. Add relevant news or representative photos when there is something useful to share. A post about a closure should accompany an update to the hours field; frequency alone does not establish a ranking bonus.

Decide Which Detail to Improve First

Start with the detail most likely to leave a customer unsure, then agree who will maintain it. Assess completed enquiries, bookings and visits separately from profile actions.
