What to Look for Before You Sign

When comparing Google Business Profile management services, begin with three questions: will you keep ownership, what work will be done, and how will the results be explained? A useful proposal should make all three clear before you sign.

Why Consider Help with a Business Profile?

A customer may find a restaurant, clinic, salon or academy on Google Maps before contacting it. The profile helps them check whether the service, location and opening hours fit their plans. Keeping that information accurate takes attention, particularly across several branches.

Google describes local ranking through a combination of:

  • Relevance: how well the business matches the search.
  • Distance: how far it is from the searcher or the location Google understands.
  • Prominence or popularity: how well known the business is, including information such as links and reviews.

You cannot pay Google for a better organic local ranking. An agency’s fee pays for agreed work, rather than a reserved map position. Useful management makes the information and customer journey better; visibility alone is not a confirmed sale.

What Should the Management Service Include?

“Management” can mean very different things in two quotations. Ask which of the following tasks are included, what needs your approval and what the agency needs from you.

1. Business Information

  • Maintain the accurate real-world name, category, address, contact information and hours.
  • Update relevant holidays, temporary closures and special opening hours.
  • Organize supported services, menus and attributes without claiming unavailable features or services.

2. Content

  • Prepare relevant updates, announcements, offers or events, subject to the applicable post rules and review.
  • Add appropriate photos and videos and replace information when the business changes.
  • Plan language support for the actual audience through supported profile fields and suitable website content. Do not assume a profile provides a separate editable description for every language.

3. Reviews and Customer Questions

  • Agree on review-reply guidelines, approval requirements and who handles a sensitive complaint.
  • Identify possible policy violations and explain reporting options, without promising to remove a negative opinion.
  • Prepare accurate answers to common customer questions for the channels actually available. Ask the agency to confirm current question-management features; Google discontinued its former Business Profile Q&A API on November 3, 2025.

4. Reporting

  • Explain the profile views or impressions available in the chosen reporting source, rather than mixing differently defined totals.
  • Report call-button clicks, direction requests and website clicks separately from completed calls, arrivals or inquiries.
  • Review available discovery terms, noting the reporting period and any data limitations.
  • Provide a monthly explanation of the work, results and next useful changes.

Seven Questions to Ask an Agency

1. Will You Keep Control of the Profile?

Google’s third-party policy says the business must retain ownership or co-ownership. A practical arrangement is to keep the business’s own account as owner and invite the agency with suitable manager access. Google allows the business to decide the partner’s role; your password is not the invitation mechanism.

2. What Happens When the Service Ends?

Google requires a quick way to discontinue the relationship. Within seven business days of notice, the client must be able to separate the managing account from the service and regain exclusive control, with the provider relinquishing its permissions as applicable.

Ask separately what files, reports and creative assets will be handed over, on what schedule and under what usage terms. Those details belong in the agreement; Google’s access rule is not a universal promise about every contract asset or refund.

3. Does the Proposal Make Unsupported Guarantees?

Be wary of “number one guaranteed” or “permanently fixed at the top.” Ask which work is within the agency’s control and which outcomes depend on Google, competitors and customer behavior. Payment for management should be disclosed clearly; the Business Profile service itself is available without an extra Google charge.

4. Can You Tell Exactly What Will Be Done?

  • How many posts or updates are planned each week or month?
  • How quickly will a review be assessed, drafted and, where required, approved?
  • How many photos will be prepared or uploaded, and who supplies them?
  • How often will you receive a report and an explanation?

These numbers help compare workload and fees. They are service commitments, not Google ranking quotas. Distinguish a promised draft-response time from the time needed for client approval or platform review.

5. Does Reporting Connect Attention with Customer Actions?

A rise in views is worth understanding, but ask what happened next. Call clicks, direction requests and website visits answer different questions. Where measurement permits, review completed inquiries or bookings separately and avoid attributing every change to profile management alone.

Google notes that available profile performance can include organic results and advertising activity. Use matching dates, definitions and sources, especially if an agency also manages ads.

6. Does the Plan Fit Your Actual Business?

A clinic needs different information from a café, and two branches may have different access or booking arrangements. Ask how the proposed work reflects your services, languages, customer questions and location. A template can organize work, but the facts must belong to your business.

7. How Does the Agency Handle Policy Requirements?

Look for accurate categories and business names, permissions for photos, truthful replies and a clear review-request policy. Incentives, positive-only requests, pressure and requested review wording are not acceptable shortcuts. A reporting process should also distinguish a possible violation from a review the business simply dislikes.

How to Compare the Cost

Fees depend on the work, number of locations and complexity. “Scope × locations × difficulty” is a way to think about the quotation, rather than a literal industry pricing formula.

Five Factors That Change the Workload

  • The number of branches and profiles.
  • The volume and sensitivity of reviews and customer questions.
  • Whether posts, photography and other content must be created or only uploaded.
  • The languages and connected website information involved.
  • The depth of reporting and recommendations.

Five Details to Check in the Quotation

  • Whether initial setup is included or charged separately.
  • The monthly tasks, quantities and responsibilities.
  • Additional charges for photography, translation or advertising work.
  • The contract term, cancellation process and any applicable charges.
  • Permission removal, information access and the agreed handover.

The lowest price is difficult to assess when the service is unclear. Compare the specific work you need and the evidence you will receive, without assuming a higher fee guarantees a higher return.

What to Prepare Before a Consultation

You do not need a finished marketing strategy. A short, accurate brief is enough to make the conversation more useful.

  • The current profile URL and any known access issue.
  • A few relevant location-and-service search examples; the original suggested five, not a required minimum.
  • The recent review situation, using the past three months as a possible starting period.
  • The action you want to improve: calls, directions, bookings, inquiries or visits, with their different meanings.
  • A realistic monthly budget range.

A simple brief can use these prompts:

  • Business name.
  • Location and service area.
  • Main services or products.
  • The problem you want help with.
  • Relevant competitors, perhaps three examples.
  • Your three-month work and measurement goals, without treating them as guaranteed outcomes.

Frequently Asked Questions

When Might I See a Difference?

The original suggested reviewing trends over four to twelve weeks. Treat that as an operating checkpoint, not a guaranteed ranking or sales timetable. Profile approval, customer demand, the starting information and the work completed all affect what can reasonably be assessed.

Should I Give the Agency My Account Password?

Use the profile’s owner and manager invitation controls instead. Keep ownership with the business and provide only the access the agreed work requires. Record how access will be removed when the relationship ends.

Is a Cheap Management Service Necessarily Better Value?

Compare what is included and what you can handle yourself. A modest service may fit a simple need; a quotation that omits necessary work can leave the original problem unresolved. Price alone does not establish value.

Does a New Business with Few Reviews Need an Agency?

It may be able to do the basics itself. An agency can help where time, access or expertise is missing, but a new profile does not automatically require a retainer. Begin with eligibility, accurate details, useful photos and optional requests for genuine feedback.

If I Already Use Naver Place, Should I Also Manage Google?

Consider where the customers you want actually search, including foreign visitors and people using Maps. Keep both accurate where they matter. Different platforms and actions should be measured separately rather than assuming one will reproduce the other’s results.

Choose a Service You Can Assess

A worthwhile management service leaves you with reliable information, retained control and a clear explanation of what has been done. Before comparing prices, check:

  • Ownership and appropriate access.
  • A defined scope and approval process.
  • Reporting that distinguishes visibility, actions and completed outcomes.
  • Policy handling and a practical handover.
