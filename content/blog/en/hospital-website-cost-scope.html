Comparing hospital website quotes by their totals alone can hide work that has been left out. Even “10 service pages” can mean different things depending on whether the hospital supplies all the materials or the agency develops the copy and designs new layouts. Before comparing prices, check the work covered by each item and the costs that continue after launch.

Check page count and layout design separately

Page count gives you one indication of the workload, but it does not define the whole project. Filling a shared template with different service information is different from designing a distinct layout for each page. Ask the agency to specify the total pages, individually designed layouts, shared templates and mobile work in the quote.

Agreeing the menu before requesting quotes makes comparison easier. Decide how much detail you need for medical team profiles, services, equipment and directions. If you need branch pages or a multilingual site, check translation, separate editing and language-switching functions individually. Look beyond the words “responsive design included”: agree which screens need specific mobile adjustments and which devices will be used for testing.

Copywriting, photography and content migration are separate tasks

Moving existing introductory text is different from developing new copy for each service. Clarify whether the agency handles content planning, writing, changes following medical team review and image editing. If photography is needed, the quote should show the location, subjects, number of edited photographs and conditions for additional shoots. The hospital should name the person who confirms medical information and set a schedule for providing materials.

For a redesign, the amount of existing content and images to move also affects the work. List posts and attachments to migrate and who will sort out outdated material. Ask the agency to distinguish automated transfer from manual work. Pages whose addresses change also need a review of where their old URLs should lead. Commissioning URL migration does not mean you can be promised unchanged search rankings.

Compare search-related work through tasks and verification records

A single “SEO and AI search optimization” line does not explain what is included. Ask whether it covers organizing service information, writing page titles and descriptions, checking search access settings and preparing a sitemap. If structured data is proposed, clarify which information and pages it will cover and what verification records you will receive. Implementation alone does not guarantee a particular search appearance or inclusion in an AI answer.

Also agree who writes the information and who reviews it. Development work does not automatically include copywriting or regular updates. Google's guidance on hiring an SEO recommends considering professional input early when planning a new website or redesign. At the quote stage, decide whether this work belongs in the initial plan or will be commissioned separately after the pages have been built.

Separate the production fee from recurring operating costs

Ask for a separate table of costs that continue after launch, such as hosting, domain renewal, a CMS subscription, paid plugins and text messages. For each item, check who pays, the renewal cycle, the included usage and any overage conditions. Compare whether VAT is included and when discounts or free items limited to the initial contract period end.

For booking integration, separate external service fees from website development costs. A simple link to an external booking page is different from an integration that exchanges information within the website. Do not assume that paying hosting or CMS fees establishes ownership or transfer rights for custom source code. Check source-code delivery and third-party license conditions separately in the contract.

Agree what triggers an additional charge

Requests to expand a menu, add features or change an agreed design after signing may require a further quote. Rather than asking only how many revision rounds are included, clarify how the agency distinguishes draft revisions, development fault fixes and new functions. Agree a process in which additional work starts after the hospital approves its cost and timing.

Finally, check where pre-launch acceptance ends and ongoing maintenance begins. The fault-fix period, routine editing scope and help transferring materials at the end of the contract are separate conditions. Mark each item in competing quotes as included, excluded or undecided. This helps define the work to include and what the hospital will handle before discussing the total price.
