“We know search matters, but what should we fix first?”

For a business owner or marketing manager, a long list of SEO advice is not always useful. A better starting point is a checklist you can apply to the site you actually manage, then use to agree priorities with whoever maintains it.

Why Use a Google SEO Checklist?

Search visibility needs occasional review as your site, customer questions and search services change. A related checklist video (Korean) accompanies the original article. No checklist can account for every site or guarantee a ranking, but it can make the work easier to discuss.

A useful list gives you a way to:

  • Check important areas without relying on memory.
  • Ask a contractor what has been checked and what evidence supports the result.
  • Choose a starting task even if you are new to SEO.

The five areas below follow the original article’s sequence. Current guidance is distinguished from the Korean article’s March 2026 publication date where something has changed.

Step 1: Check the Basics

Begin by checking whether important public pages can be reached, understood and used. A useful page needs to work for the customer as well as be accessible to search engines.

  • Verify the site in Google Search Console. It gives authorized owners access to Google’s indexing and search-performance reports. It is a useful diagnostic tool, rather than a prerequisite for Google to discover a site.
  • Check and submit the sitemap. A sitemap helps Google discover relevant URLs. Submission is a hint, not a promise that new pages will be indexed more quickly or at all.
  • Check robots.txt. Look at example.com/robots.txt and relevant Disallow rules. Avoid unintentionally blocking pages you want crawled. A crawl block is not a reliable way to keep a URL out of the index; indexing controls require their own appropriate setup.
  • Use HTTPS correctly. Keep certificates valid, redirect intended HTTP URLs appropriately and avoid insecure resources. Give customers a working secure connection rather than treating one security setting as a ranking guarantee.
  • Check the mobile version. Google uses mobile content for indexing. Important text, links and functions should remain available and usable on a phone; a visual issue is something to diagnose, not evidence of an automatic fixed ranking penalty.
A starting task: if you have the right access, begin setting up Search Console. The original five-minute estimate is only a planning allowance; ownership verification and initial reporting can take longer.

When you review performance, separate Search impressions and clicks from website sessions, inquiries and completed sales. They answer different questions about how the site is working.

Step 2: Make the Content Easier to Understand

Good content answers the reader’s question accurately. Clear titles, headings and links help people navigate that answer; they do not replace it.

  • Write a descriptive title. Include the real topic naturally. The original 60-character suggestion can guide editing, but Google has no fixed title-character limit and may truncate or generate a different title link.
  • Write a useful meta description. Summarize the particular page accurately. The original 155-character target is an editorial convention, not a Google limit or a guaranteed displayed snippet. Google may use text from the page instead.
  • Use clear headings. One obvious main heading followed by meaningful subheadings is a sensible structure. Multiple H1 elements are not, by themselves, a published automatic ranking penalty.
  • Use the language of the topic naturally. Explain the actual service and relevant questions. Do not stuff phrases into every paragraph to meet a keyword frequency or beginning-middle-end formula.
  • Add appropriate image alternatives. Use the alt attribute to describe meaningful images in context. Purely decorative images can use empty alt text. A descriptive filename can help organization, but is not a substitute for useful alternatives or a traffic guarantee.
  • Link to relevant pages. Help a reader reach the next useful explanation, service or booking information. Two or three links per article is a planning suggestion, not a minimum Google requires.

Step 3: Check Local Business Information

If you serve a particular area, make the place and service clear. A search for a restaurant in Gangnam or an interior contractor in Busan should lead to information about the actual business, not a page that merely repeats a place name.

  • Check the Google Business Profile. Add or claim the correct eligible business, verify it as required and maintain hours, contact details, photos and accurate categories.
  • Check name, address and phone number. Make sure Google, Naver, KakaoMap and your website identify the same business and branch. Correct material conflicts; small formatting differences do not establish an automatic trust penalty.
  • Explain the real service area. Mention useful local details naturally in the page’s content and title where relevant, without adding unrelated location keywords to the business name.
  • Look after genuine reviews. Invite honest feedback without selecting only satisfied customers, offering rewards or requesting specific wording. Read concerns and reply where useful. Reviews and ratings are among several local factors, not a complete ranking formula.
Do not buy or fabricate Google reviews. Google’s policies prohibit fake engagement and incentivized reviews, and violations can lead to removals or account and profile restrictions. An honest review must remain the customer’s own choice.

Step 4: Check Technical Issues That Affect the Experience

You can identify questions worth asking even if a developer will do the repair. Let the page and its reports show you where to investigate first.

  • Review performance. Use PageSpeed Insights to understand the findings for an actual URL. The original score of 60 is not an official ranking cutoff. Prioritize the real loading or interaction problem rather than chasing one laboratory score.
  • Check Core Web Vitals. Review LCP, INP and CLS in the available field reports. Some pages have insufficient real-user data; that is different from passing or failing.
  • Check relevant structured data. It must match visible content and a currently supported feature. Correct markup does not guarantee rich results. FAQ rich results ended on May 7, 2026, and self-serving LocalBusiness or Organization reviews are not eligible for review stars simply because a business marks up its own testimonials.
  • Check broken links and missing pages. Repair links that should lead to live content. A genuinely removed page can correctly return 404 or 410; do not redirect every missing URL to the homepage merely to eliminate a report entry.

Step 5: Put Current Search Advice in Context

Some advice in an older checklist can become dated before the rest does. These three areas deserve particular care.

AI Overviews and Other AI Search Features

Answer the reader’s question clearly and provide useful supporting detail. Do not assume AI Overviews appear for most queries or that an early answer paragraph guarantees citation. Google says supporting links must be indexed and eligible for a Search snippet; ordinary SEO practices apply, with no special AI schema required. FAQ markup does not guarantee AI citation, and the former FAQ rich-result feature is no longer available.

E-E-A-T: Make Experience and Responsibility Clear

E-E-A-T means Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness and Trustworthiness. Google says it is not a single specific ranking factor. Describe real experience where it is relevant, explain who is responsible for the information and support important claims. An author page should describe actual involvement and qualifications. Do not invent “we tried it” accounts or customer results to imitate firsthand evidence.

What the Original Article Called an “Authenticity Update”

The Korean article used this label for a supposed change beginning in the second half of 2025. We could not substantiate that named update from the cited material or Google’s official guidance. The practical advice can be stated without it: publish useful, reliable content and avoid producing pages at scale primarily to manipulate rankings. Google’s scaled-content policy applies regardless of whether people, AI or a combination produced the material. AI use alone does not establish that a page is low quality.

Focus on a clear answer, evidence the reader can assess and a page that works well. Structure and genuine experience help explain the information; neither is a shortcut that guarantees visibility or AI citation.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does SEO take?

The original article suggested three to six months, with one to two months for some less competitive topics and six months or longer for others. Treat these as planning ranges, not a forecast. The change, site history, crawling, demand and competition affect what happens. Agree which measures you will review and separate indexing from clicks, inquiries and sales.

How often should I use the checklist?

A weekly check of important notices and customer information can be useful, with a monthly or fortnightly review of content and technical reports. The original weekly local/monthly basic/fortnightly-or-monthly technical schedule is a suggested routine. Adjust it to your publishing activity, business changes and any actual errors; it is not a Google compliance timetable.

Should I do this myself or hire help?

You can often begin with accurate information, clearer writing and checking the reports you already have access to. Bring in suitable help where a technical issue, research task or maintenance workload exceeds your time or skills. Ask what will be changed, why it matters and how the result will be checked.

Choose One Task to Begin With

You now have five areas to review. Begin with the most important gap on the actual site rather than trying to complete every item today.

If you lack Search Console access, establish the appropriate ownership or permission route. If a page title is vague, make it accurately describe the page. Check the result of that task before moving to the next.
