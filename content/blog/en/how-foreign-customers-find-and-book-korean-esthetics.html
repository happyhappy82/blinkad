Marketing a Korean esthetic salon to international customers means helping them understand the service, check current prices and duration, and book through a channel where someone actually replies. Photographs may attract attention, but the decision can stall if service names appear only in Korean or prices and booking instructions are hard to find.

Here, “esthetic salon” means a non-medical skincare business, rather than medical aesthetics provided by a hospital or clinic. Article 14 of Korea's Enforcement Rule of the Public Health Control Act (Korean) defines the work of licensed skincare beauticians as skin-condition analysis, skincare, hair removal and eyebrow grooming without medical devices or medicines. Article 27 of the Medical Service Act (Korean) prohibits medical practice by non-medical practitioners. Check the business's actual license and permitted scope first, then keep service names and descriptions within it.

How do international customers find an esthetic salon?

A first-time visitor may combine an area with the service they want before knowing a business name. After discovery through Maps or search, they look at photographs and reviews, compare service details and prices, then move to consultation or booking.

What belongs in a service description?

Names such as “contouring care,” “hydration care” or “special care” may be familiar to Korean customers but unclear to someone visiting from abroad. Go beyond translating the name: bring the information needed to choose into one place.

Google's local business link guidance covers links to service lists and appointment booking. Available editing fields can vary by business type and account. Open the current profile to see which service and booking fields it provides.

How should customer photographs and consultation information be handled?

Esthetic content can include a customer's face or body. Before taking identifiable photographs and using them in promotion, define the purpose, publication channels and period, and establish the necessary consent process. Article 15 of the Personal Information Protection Act (Korean) sets out grounds for collecting and using personal information and the information to provide when obtaining consent.

Where and how a photograph is published must match the consent obtained. A photograph supplied for internal training should not simply be reused in website or social promotion. Manage publication channels and periods separately. Assign someone to handle deletion requests and record where original and edited files are stored.

Define which skin-condition or health details are necessary for booking. Move those discussions from public comments to a channel the salon manages, with internal rules for access and retention. Do not infer sensitive details customers have not supplied or build separate lists of them to measure marketing performance.

How should a language-specific service page be organized?

Use one page for one service or a clearly differentiated group. Start by answering the area covered, scope, duration and how to check the price, then explain pre-booking checks and changes. Review website languages and booking with the international-customer website checklist (Korean).

Avoid carrying over Korean-only abbreviations or fashionable terms unchanged. Put a plain description first, followed by the salon's program name. State the languages, channels and hours staff can actually support.

For multiple languages, provide separate URLs and clear language-switching links. Google's multilingual-site guidance explains language-specific URLs and hreflang. When a service or price changes, update the Korean operational source and translations together and show the last check date. In our reviews, even a naturally written translation can still show an old price or outdated opening hours. We compare each item alongside the Korean source and record the change date.

What makes a page useful for search and AI?

Use real customer questions as subheadings and answer them in the first sentence. Choose questions the salon can directly verify: what the service includes, how long it takes, or which channel offers English consultation.

Google's guide to generative AI search optimization emphasizes useful, distinctive information and firsthand experience over making many pages for variations of the same question. For an esthetic salon, publish the actual service scope, booking process, entrance and service-room photographs, and common questions received by the operator rather than repeating general skincare explanations.

Which marketing results should an esthetic salon check first?

Separate profile views, website clicks, directions, inquiries, confirmed appointments and attendance. Google Business Profile performance guidance describes views, clicks and applicable interactions for verified profiles. Start with the direction, call and website-click metrics actually available.

On the booking site, distinguish sessions, service-detail views, booking starts and completions. In salon records, check actual attendance excluding cancellations. Do not add Maps website clicks to bookings or treat a social-media inquiry as a completed reservation.

Record the starting figures alongside promotions, price changes, closing days and seasonal factors. After improving a service description, for example, examine entries to that service's booking page and changes in repeated questions, as well as views. If profile views rise without inquiries, revisit service explanations and booking links. If directions and attendance differ substantially, check entrance guidance, hours and confirmation messages together.

Frequently asked questions

What can we show instead of before-and-after photographs?

Start with the entrance, waiting area, service room and photographs that explain the actual service sequence. When showing products or tools, explain what is included in the current service. For identifiable customers, define the intended use and publication scope and check the necessary consent process.

How should we describe language support?

List the languages, channels and times staff can actually support. If translation tools assist, define the human check and confirmation process. Customers need to know where their appointment will be confirmed, beyond whether translation is possible.

What should we prepare first to help customers find the salon on Maps?

Align the category with the business, and prepare the correct address, hours, current services and prices. Add photographs of representative services and the facilities, then check that service pages and booking links open correctly on a phone.
