At a glance
Appearing on Google Maps does not automatically produce inquiries. First check which searches bring up the business, then separate what people do after viewing the profile: calling, requesting directions, visiting the website or booking. If views are high but inquiries are absent, inspect the photographs, reviews, service descriptions and contact links before increasing advertising spend.

Search for the business name on your own phone and the profile appears clearly. Yet calls, bookings and direction requests barely change. That is when an owner starts asking, “We are on Google Maps, so why is nobody getting in touch?”

This does not necessarily mean the listing was set up incorrectly. Someone who already knows the business name may see a different set of results from a new customer searching by area and business type. Start with what customers searched for and what they clicked next.

A business-name search does not show whether new customers can find you

Existing customers can type the exact name. New customers do not know it yet. They search for what they need using combinations such as area plus business type or area plus service, then compare several businesses.

Google explains that local results are based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence. Google: improving local ranking Results for the same business can differ with the query and the searcher's location. One search for the business name is not enough to judge visibility.

For a Korean-owned business outside Korea, Korean queries alone are not enough either. Local-language searches, English searches and the Korean phrases used by the local Korean community can differ. Break the journey from views to inquiries into four stages

Google Business Profile performance can show search terms, views, calls, direction requests and website clicks. Performance is available for verified profiles, and the metrics shown vary with the business and account. Google Business Profile: understanding performance

Look for the stage where the numbers fall, rather than combining them:

StageWarning signCheck first
Search → viewFew relevant search terms or viewsCategories, service descriptions and visibility by area and language
View → actionMany views, few clicksMain photograph, opening hours, reviews, menus and service information
Action → inquiryWebsite clicks without contactMobile page, language, contact buttons and booking page
Inquiry → booking or visitCalls or messages, but few bookingsResponse time, information provided and how bookings are confirmed

This table does not establish a cause. Use it to decide what to check first, rather than assuming that every problem comes from low visibility.

Does the profile answer a first-time customer's questions?

A new customer compares businesses quickly. The profile helps them decide what a business offers, whether it is open, where to check prices or conditions, and whether they can find the location.

Google Business Profile lets you manage the address, hours, contact details, website and photographs. Google: editing business information What matters is whether those details match the business now, not simply whether every field is filled in.

Apply that test to photographs too. A restaurant should show representative dishes and the actual space; a salon, its work and interior; a grocery store, its product range and entrance. A consultation-based business needs to explain the service scope and where to visit. For a café example, see how international tourists save and visit Korean cafés.

Try the call and booking links yourself

A profile can have links that are still difficult to use. The phone number may be outdated, the website may fail on mobile, or the booking button may lead to another branch.

Depending on the business type and region, Google Business Profile supports links for actions such as viewing a menu, booking or ordering. Google: local business links These links should reach a dedicated page for the relevant location where the customer can complete the stated action. Google: business link policies

Go beyond checking the URL in the administration screen. Tap the link on the mobile page customers see. Check whether it opens the local-language page, whether the form is unnecessarily long, and whether a confirmation message arrives after booking. Look at recent review content and replies, as well as the count

A large review count may still leave new customers' questions unanswered if the reviews describe services no longer offered. Check whether recent reviews discuss the current menu or services, location and booking experience.

Google allows businesses to request reviews based on real experiences, but prohibits discounts or free products in exchange for writing or changing a review. Google: getting reviews Give actual customers an official review link or QR code, and reply briefly and specifically to the reviews they leave.

Replies do not need to read like long advertisements. Respond to the menu item, service or visit the customer mentioned. Move complicated complaints to a separate contact channel so personal details are not exposed.

Check the inquiry process before adding advertising

Advertising can create more opportunities to appear in search, but it cannot repair a disconnected phone number or an awkward booking page. If the profile already receives plenty of views, inspect the last screen a customer uses to contact you before adding ads.

If relevant searches produce almost no views, review the categories, service descriptions, local-language pages and Maps competition before deciding the advertising scope. Once ads are running, compare calls, direction requests and website clicks with actual inquiry and booking records as well as views.

Frequently asked questions

If we rank first for our business name, is our visibility already good?

You may be easy to find for people who know the exact name. Whether new customers can find you through area, business-type and service searches needs a separate check.

What should we check first if views are high but there are no calls?

Tap the phone number from the customer-facing profile and check that the call connects. Then look at whether the main photograph, hours, service descriptions and reviews give customers enough reason to call.

Should we start Google Ads when inquiries are low?

First check that the call, website and booking links work, and that profile details match current operations. Advertising with a broken contact process may increase clicks without producing actual inquiries.
