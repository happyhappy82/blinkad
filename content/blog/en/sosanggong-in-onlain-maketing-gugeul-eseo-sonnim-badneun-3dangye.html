“How can I bring in customers when I barely have a marketing budget?”

For a small business, even a modest advertising bill can feel substantial. The original article described Naver clicks costing hundreds to thousands of won and Instagram campaigns requiring several hundred thousand won a month. Those were illustrative costs, rather than a current price survey. There is another place to begin: help people already searching on Google find accurate information about your business. That can create opportunities without a large media budget, although a particular number of monthly customers is never assured. Here are three steps you can work through at your own pace.

Why Include Google in Your Small Business Marketing?

If most of your customers use Naver, Google may seem like an extra task. We suggest looking at how people actually find your shop before deciding how much time to give each platform.

  • The Korean article cited Google approaching 40% of Korea’s search market. Its measurement period and comparable source series were not supplied, so treat that as historical context rather than a current market-share figure.
  • Customers in their 20s to 40s may use Google Search and Maps, but age alone does not establish which app your customers prefer.
  • Searches such as “restaurants nearby” or “nail salon in Gangnam” can display local map results. Their presence and position depend on the query and location.
  • An accurate Google Business Profile is a useful starting point for an eligible business serving customers in person.

Naver SmartPlace still deserves attention. Google may offer an additional way to be found, especially where your target customers already use it. Competition needs to be checked in your own area; a quieter market cannot be assumed.

The Korean article described a small bakery in Mapo, Seoul, reporting more than 20% growth in visits attributed to Google Maps after three months of profile work. The underlying visit records were not supplied with the article. Read it as an account from the original publication, rather than a verified case study or a forecast for your shop.

Step 1: Set Up and Complete Your Google Business Profile

Start with the information a customer needs before visiting: what you do, where you are, and when you are open. Google Business Profile lets an eligible owner manage that information on Search and Maps.

How to Get Started

  • Go to business.google.com and sign in with the Google Account you will use for the business.
  • Find an existing listing before adding a new one. Add or claim the correct business, using its real-world name, category and accurate address or service area.
  • Complete the verification method Google offers your profile. Options vary; phone and postcard are not available to everyone. Google says its review can take up to five business days after the verification steps, occasionally longer. A mailed code, where offered, usually arrives within 14 days.

The Details Worth Getting Right

Complete information helps customers make a decision and can help Google match the business to relevant searches. The Korean article cited a 70% higher likelihood of a visit, but did not identify the underlying study. We have not used that percentage as a current result or a ranking guarantee. Google explains its main local-ranking factors as relevance, distance and prominence.
  • Categories: choose the most accurate primary category and add only categories that describe the business. The original suggestion of two or three secondary categories is a planning idea, not a required quota.
  • Hours: enter regular hours and keep holidays, temporary closures and breaks accurate.
  • Photos: show the entrance, interior and representative products or dishes. Ten useful photos can be a manageable starting plan; Google does not publish a ten-photo ranking threshold.
  • Description: stay within 750 characters and explain your actual business and location naturally. A shop does not need the phrase “small business online marketing” in its description.
  • Services or menu: add the relevant offerings and accurate prices where those features are available. Give customers enough information to decide whether you offer what they need.

Step 2: Make Honest Reviews Easy to Leave

Reviews help people assess an unfamiliar business. Google says review volume and positive ratings can contribute to local ranking, alongside other factors. You can make the process convenient without trying to control what customers say.

Simple Ways to Invite Reviews

  • A review QR code: put a clearly labeled code linking to the correct Google review page at the counter or on a table. Let customers choose whether and when to use it.
  • A thank-you message: where you are permitted to contact the customer, include an optional invitation to share an honest review after the visit.
  • A brief staff invitation: “If you’d like to share your experience, our Google review link is here.” Avoid pressure, requested wording and staff review quotas.

How to Handle the Reviews You Receive

  • Read and respond thoughtfully. Thank customers where a reply adds something, and address concerns with a useful explanation or next step.
  • Write for the customer. A specific, natural reply is more helpful than repeating location and service keywords; Google does not promise a ranking boost from keywords in owner replies.
  • Respond calmly to criticism and protect the customer’s privacy. A low rating alone is not grounds for removal, but a review that violates policy can be reported.

Step 3: Keep Customers Informed with Small, Regular Updates

Once the basics are in place, keep them useful. A new menu item, changed opening time or seasonal photo gives a customer a reason to look again. Posting regularly is an operating habit, rather than a published Google ranking requirement.

A Manageable Posting Routine

  • Consider one or two posts a week if you have worthwhile news. Adjust the schedule to the business.
  • Include a relevant photo when it helps show the dish, product or event.
  • Use an available action button or link that takes the customer to the appropriate website, booking page or other supported destination.
  • Keep the message short and clear. The original suggestion of roughly 200 characters is an editorial guideline, not a platform requirement.

A Realistic Timeline for Getting Started

Plan the tasks you can control, then watch how customers respond. Verification, local competition and demand can all affect the pace.

Week 1: begin registration and verification; prepare accurate details and photos. Verification may extend beyond the first week.
Month 1: check visibility and customer actions. The original five-to-ten-review suggestion is not a staff quota or a requirement for success. Invite honest feedback without incentives.
Month 3: compare the same measures over time. Twenty reviews, stable top positions and regular monthly visits are not automatic three-month milestones.

A Few Habits That Make the Work Easier

  • Keep Naver SmartPlace up to date too. Two well-managed listings can reach people using different services, although their audiences overlap and coverage does not simply double.
  • Refresh photos when something changes. Show the entrance customers will actually see and the products currently available.
  • Answer common questions where customers can read them. Use the profile features available in your region and keep useful answers on your website as well.
  • Review Business Profile performance each month. Search terms, website clicks, call-button clicks and direction requests describe different actions. A click or request does not confirm a completed visit or sale.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can any business use Google Business Profile?

The service is free, but eligibility matters. A business generally needs to make in-person contact with customers, either at its premises or by visiting them. An online-only business is not eligible simply because its owner has a Google Account.

Do I need Google if I already manage Naver SmartPlace?

Consider where your customers look for you. Google can be useful for both Korean and international customers, while Naver also offers multilingual maps and translated reviews. Avoid assuming that every foreign visitor uses Google or that one platform is always less competitive. Start with correct information on both, then use your own inquiry and visit records to set priorities.

How do I begin when I have very few reviews?

Invite genuine customers to describe their experience in their own words. A regular customer can leave an honest review, but friends, relatives, staff and others with a conflict of interest should not be recruited to boost ratings. Offer no money, gifts, discounts or free services. Five reviews is not a published threshold; focus on a fair process that welcomes positive and negative feedback alike.
