When a clinic asks about attracting international patients, the first question is often how much to spend on advertising. Before discussing that budget, we look at how the clinic appears when a prospective patient searches. That starting point helps us decide what to address first.

A Gangnam dermatology clinic: 25 locations, three keyword checks

The clinic in this case was near Dosan-daero in Gangnam. At the time, its Google rating was 5.0 out of 5. That was a positive review signal, rather than a measure of clinical outcomes. The assessment used a 5×5 grid of 25 search locations around the clinic and checked one Korean, one English and one Japanese keyword at each location: 75 keyword-location observations, not 75 different locations.

The original description gives an approximate 480-metre grid setting, without clearly distinguishing radius from spacing. We have not reconstructed those coordinates for this edition. The important point is that map rankings can vary by search location: a clinic may appear near its entrance but rank differently near a hotel a few blocks away. A grid shows more of that variation than a single search.

Why did a 5.0 rating not translate into strong map visibility?

The clinic had seven Google reviews at the time. Some competitors ranking well for the Korean keyword had hundreds of reviews and ratings around 4.8–4.9. Google describes local results in terms of relevance, distance and prominence; review count and positive ratings can contribute. A high rating alone does not settle the ranking, and this comparison does not prove that review count caused the gap.

The source also described leading competitors with multilingual information on their profiles. It reported top-three visibility figures of 88% for the leading English-search competitor and 84% for the leading Japanese-search competitor. These are the original assessment's reported visibility metrics, not shares of patients, traffic or all available ranking positions. The full competitor reports are not reproduced here, so we have not recalculated those percentages.

Those observations make profile accuracy and useful language support worth checking. They do not prove that adding a language to a profile name improves rankings. A business name should reflect the real-world name used by the clinic, in line with Google's guidelines; unrelated keywords or invented name variations are not a remedy.

Why we diagnose visibility before planning ads

International patients may use Google, Maps or an AI service such as ChatGPT to research a clinic. The original article described map cards in some agency-observed AI answers showing business names and ratings. That is an observation of particular answers, not a universal account of where every AI service gets its information. Map visibility and AI mentions need to be checked separately.

Consider a patient who clicks an ad, then looks up the clinic on Maps before making an inquiry. They may compare its information and reviews with nearby alternatives. An incomplete or unconvincing profile can leave questions unanswered, even when the ad generated a click. This case does not measure wasted ad spend or prove that advertising would fail; it explains why we recommend diagnosis, basic improvements and then an advertising plan.

What a useful diagnosis should check

  • The clinic's map positions for selected target-language keywords, measured across relevant locations rather than at one point.
  • Which clinics appear in those results, and the differences in their accurate business information, review counts and language support.
  • Whether selected AI services mention the clinic when asked relevant patient questions, with the language, date and conditions recorded.
  • Whether prospective patients can understand the clinic's name, category, photographs and booking links.

These checks give the team a more concrete basis for setting priorities. In the case described here, profile information and a process for receiving genuine reviews deserved attention before increasing ad spend. A later comparison should use comparable keywords, locations and settings, record the new date and keep map positions separate from inquiries, bookings and visits.
