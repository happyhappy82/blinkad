You may want to update your hospital website for AI search without knowing how much of the existing site you can keep. An older design may concern you, while the cost of starting again may feel difficult to justify.

Start with the work that needs doing, rather than the site's age or appearance. Updating copy and links, adding functions and redesigning the whole site involve different project scopes.

Turn the problems into specific tasks

Descriptions such as “the website feels cluttered” or “it seems poorly suited to AI search” give an agency little basis for a quote. Record which screen causes a problem and what needs to change.

For example, outdated medical team information calls for checking the current facts and revising the copy. If service information exists only in images, check whether body text can be added. A contact button that is too small or leads to the wrong page needs changes to the layout or link.

If you cannot add a required page, or need to connect a new inquiry and intake system, review the development scope separately. One website may need both content updates and functional development.

First check what can be changed on the current site

Find out what your staff can edit through the administrator interface and what the developer can change. The hospital's inability to make an edit does not by itself mean a rebuild is necessary. In some cases, checking the editing scope and permissions in the current contract may resolve the problem.

Search issues need actual inspection records. Google processes JavaScript pages through crawling, rendering and indexing. Before deciding on a complete rebuild because of a particular technology, check whether the necessary text and links are being delivered.

If titles, body text, links and search settings can be changed on the existing site, and the results can be checked afterward, start by requesting a quote for that work.

When should you compare additional development with a rebuild?

Compare the two when the current site cannot accommodate new pages or language versions, or when a needed inquiry function is difficult to connect to the existing system.

Ask about ongoing operation as well as whether the feature can be built. Will staff be able to update the information afterward? Could it conflict with the current booking system? Who will maintain it?

If the issue is missing account access or source materials, first review the contract and handover scope. For a site using a hosted service or external solution, ask separately what can be transferred and which fees will continue.

Compare both quotes against the same completion criteria

Assess partial-update and rebuild quotes against the same hospital requirements. For example:

  • Will the necessary service, medical team and first-visit information be provided?
  • Will the text and search access of public pages be checked?
  • Will the inquiry and booking process be checked on mobile?
  • If existing URLs change, is migration work included?
  • Are the hospital's editing scope and post-launch support specified?

If copywriting and search checks are included in the rebuild but missing from the update quote, the price difference alone will not tell you which is appropriate. Add the missing work before comparing.

Google also recommends discussing SEO when planning a new site or redesign. Include the required checks in both options from the start, rather than adding search work after deciding how to proceed with the website.

Account for the materials your existing site already holds

Review existing service information, medical team materials and images, along with the pages receiving search traffic, before deciding what to carry forward. Sorting materials, transferring copy, changing URLs and testing may require work beyond creating a new design.

If patient information or inquiry records are to be moved, keep that work separate from public-content migration and confirm the responsible people, access and transfer method. Do more than confirm a backup exists: clarify what can actually be restored and who is responsible in the contract.
