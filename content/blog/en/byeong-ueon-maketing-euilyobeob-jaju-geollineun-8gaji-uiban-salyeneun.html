When reviewing a clinic advertisement in Korea, it can be tempting to fix a few words and consider the job done. Would replacing “the best” or anonymizing a patient’s name resolve the problem? At BlinkAd, we recommend looking first at what the advertisement as a whole promises and what evidence supports it.

Korea’s Medical Service Act addresses both advertising content and how it is distributed. The review needs to consider possible misleading impressions about treatment, the accuracy of the information, and whether the intended medium requires prior review. Here are eight areas for clinic owners and marketing teams to check.

1. What should you check under Articles 56 and 57?

Article 56 addresses who may advertise medical services and prohibited content and methods. It covers matters including false or exaggerated claims, treatment testimonials that could mislead consumers, and omission of important adverse-effect information.

Article 57 sets out the media subject to prior review and the review procedure. Check the medium and content together. The Act also provides an exemption for advertisements consisting solely of specified basic information, such as the institution’s name, address and phone number.

Include the copy, images, intended medium and linked landing page in the production brief. The reviewer needs to understand how and where the information will appear.

2. Eight types of medical advertising to examine

1. Treatment testimonials and patient reviews

Article 56(2)(2) prohibits patient treatment testimonials and similar advertising that could mislead consumers about treatment effects. Patient consent or anonymization does not complete the review of that content.

Check consent and the permitted use of personal information alongside the expectations the advertisement creates. A satisfaction comment used as evidence of an expected treatment result also needs scrutiny.

2. Discounts and waived fees for non-covered treatment

Article 56(2)(13) addresses discounts or waivers of non-covered treatment charges advertised in ways that deceive or could mislead consumers. Check the eligible patients, included and excluded items, period and conditions against the actual offer.

Distinguish statutory insurance copayments from non-covered treatment prices. Calling an amount a “fixed price” does not resolve every other issue in the advertisement.

3. False or exaggerated outcomes and statistics

Claims such as “100% effective” or “zero side effects” need to be reviewed under the prohibitions on false and exaggerated advertising. For an internal statistic, verify the period, population, denominator and measure. Keep the stated result within the group actually studied.

When supporting records are unavailable, explain the verifiable care process and assessment steps rather than filling the gap with invented satisfaction rates or case counts.

4. Missing prior review where it is required

Check prior-review requirements before advertising through media covered by Article 57. Compare the detailed rules for internet media in the Enforcement Decree, along with any applicable content exemption.

If approved advertising is edited, check with the relevant review body whether the changes remain within the approved scope. Retain the submitted version and the advertisement actually published.

5. “Number one,” “best,” “only” and comparative claims

Article 56 includes prohibitions on comparisons with other medical practitioners’ functions or treatment methods and exaggeration of objective facts. If a ranking is mentioned, first establish what it measures and whether its organization, period and population can be identified.

Even a documented qualification or experience claim should be considered in the context of the complete advertisement. Evidence does not automatically make every presentation of it appropriate.

6. Procedure footage and before-and-after photographs

Article 56 addresses direct exposure of procedures, misleading impressions about effects, and false or exaggerated content. For before-and-after images, examine their source, permitted use, shooting conditions, edits, captions and any important omissions.

Avoid editing images to misrepresent results or presenting another institution’s material as the clinic’s own. Review the images and explanation together; a brief individual-results statement cannot replace that assessment.

7. Patient solicitation and referral arrangements

Article 27(3) prohibits specified forms of patient introduction, referral and solicitation for profit and also sets out exceptions. Examine how patients are connected and how compensation works, rather than relying only on the fee’s name in a contract.

The Act includes an exception concerning eligible foreign-patient attraction. Patient categories and contracts therefore need to be examined under the applicable provisions and any separate requirements.

8. Qualifications, titles and experience

Article 56(2)(9) prohibits qualifications or titles without a legal basis. Verify licenses, specialist qualifications, degrees and experience, and keep their different meanings clear.

Use the exact scope of an association membership or completed course. Keep supporting documents alongside the public wording so that the reviewer can assess both.

3. What should change when a questionable claim is found?

Use the table as a review guide. Editing the sentence and checking the suitability of the complete advertisement should happen together.

Material or claim Check first Editing direction
Definite claims about effects or safety Evidence and its scope Describe verified facts and conditions
A patient satisfaction testimonial Content, consent, personal information and possible misleading impressions Assess whether and how the testimonial may be used
A discounted price Actual price, inclusions, eligibility, period and applicable provisions Explain the conditions clearly
Internal satisfaction or procedure statistics Records, period, denominator and method Use only the verified scope
Before-and-after images or procedure footage Source, consent, edits, explanation and what is shown Review the material with the complete advertisement
A qualification, experience or ranking Basis and supporting records State the verified scope accurately

4. Applying for review and managing approved advertising

Consult the current application guidance from the relevant medical, dental or Korean medicine advertising review body: the Korean Medical Association, Korean Dental Association, or Association of Korean Medicine. Use the body’s current instructions for fees and required materials.

Under Article 57, approval is valid for three years from the approval date. An application is required six months before expiry if the advertising is to continue afterward. Keep the production date, approval date and published version together.

5. Preparing useful search content and the advertising review together

Explain the questions patients need answered using information the clinic can support. There is no need to fill the page with unverified outcomes or statistics. Start with the clinic’s actual scope of care, preparation process and practical guidance.

A FAQ or short-answer format does not itself exempt content from medical advertising review. Separate operational facts for the clinic team, clinical explanations for the medical reviewer, and material requiring medium-specific review. This makes each person’s task clearer.
