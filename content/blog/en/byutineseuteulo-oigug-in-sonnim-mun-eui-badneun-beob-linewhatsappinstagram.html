Where international inquiries get held up

1. Customers use different apps

Do not assume that a visitor uses KakaoTalk. Depending on their usual habits and home market, they may prefer LINE, WhatsApp or Instagram DMs. If your salon offers no familiar way to get in touch, that inquiry may never reach you.

2. They send a photograph

A customer may send a picture of a hairstyle or nail design and ask, “Can you do this?” Instagram DMs can be a useful way to have that conversation.

3. They ask shortly before visiting

Someone working around a travel itinerary may contact you a day or two beforehand, or on the day itself. By the time a late reply arrives, they may have booked somewhere else.

4. The message arrives during an appointment

You may be working with a customer when the inquiry comes in. By the time you can check your phone, the customer may already have moved on.

How BeautyNest handles the conversation

Connected channels appear in one screen

BeautyNest brings inquiries from connected Instagram DM, LINE, WhatsApp, Naver TalkTalk and KakaoTalk accounts together, with the originating channel shown in the conversation. Staff can follow the messages without opening each app separately.

The supported channel list is expanding. Before adopting the service, confirm that the accounts and channels your salon needs can currently be connected.

Staff can reply in Korean

The language of the customer's first message becomes the reply language. A staff member types in Korean, and the reply is delivered in that language. The customer's original message is displayed alongside the translation, with Simplified and Traditional Chinese distinguished.

This lets staff handle written inquiries without copying messages back and forth between a translator and a messaging app.

A prepared message can go out while you are busy

If no one replies within the time you have set, a saved acknowledgment goes out first. A message saying that the inquiry has been received and someone will reply shortly can give the customer a reason to wait. Silence gives them little indication of whether anyone is available.

Prepare answers to recurring questions

Start with the questions that are likely to come up repeatedly:

  • Do I need an appointment?
  • Can someone help me in English?
  • How long does the service take?
  • Can I pay by card?
  • How do I get there?

Writing and checking these answers in advance saves repeated translation and helps staff reply sooner.

For short-stay visitors, the experience and review matter

A traveler may find it difficult to return after the trip. That makes their visit different from a local customer's regular appointment.

A review can still help the next visitor decide. A review written in their language may make it easier to see how another customer experienced the salon, although language alone does not establish the writer's nationality.

For these customers, we suggest placing more emphasis on the experience on the day and a review request afterward than on reminders for a near-term return visit. Thank them after the service and invite honest feedback, without offering a reward.

Frequently asked questions

How can I tell whether international customers are already interested?

Look at the search terms in your Google Business Profile performance report and the languages used in reviews. They can point to interest from customers using other languages. Compare those clues with your inquiry and visit records before drawing conclusions about who is coming in.

What if none of our staff speak another language?

Staff can handle written inquiries in Korean through the translation feature. Face-to-face service is a separate matter. Preparing a few practical phrases and explanations for use in the salon can help.

Can we confirm a service from a photograph alone?

That depends on your salon's policy. Agree in advance on what a photo can establish and what needs to be checked during the visit, so staff do not make promises they cannot assess.

What happens to messages sent in the middle of the night?

An out-of-hours acknowledgment can go out first. Staff can resume the conversation when the salon reopens.

Should we give a price before the customer visits?

A traveler may be planning around a budget. Even an indicative price range can help them decide and reduce misunderstandings on arrival.

Where to begin

The channels customers can reach you on and how quickly you reply can make a substantial difference to whether an inquiry continues.

  • Use profile information and actual inquiries to understand the customers in your area.
  • Offer the channels those customers use.
  • Let staff reply in Korean while the messages are translated.
  • Set a prepared acknowledgment for inquiries outside opening hours.
  • For short-stay tourists, give the visit experience and an honest review request more attention than a near-term return reminder.
