Give Customers Reliable Information Before They Visit

Google provides the profile service without a subscription charge for eligible businesses that meet customers in person. An agency may charge separately for setup or ongoing work. Neither registration nor payment for management reserves a top position.

  • Business mix: the original classified 88.9% of businesses as local rather than franchise businesses. That does not establish how often residents search for “nearby” services or use Google.
  • Category differences: a restaurant and salon need different information. The source’s 416 restaurants and 161 hair/beauty businesses can prompt a service discussion, but do not define the difficulty of every local search.
  • A useful location answer: a phrase such as a restaurant on Chunghyeon-ro expresses the original example’s meaning. Treat it as a planning idea, then check actual customer questions and the real address.

Choose What to Update First

1. Check the Details Customers Will Rely On

  • Name and contact: use the real-world business name, precise address, phone and relevant website where available. Chunghyeon-ro, Saenae-ro and Sejong-ro should appear only where they describe the actual location, with the correct unit and entrance.
  • Hours: check the normal schedule and relevant holiday or temporary changes before customers rely on an old time.
  • Category: choose the most accurate available primary category and only relevant additional ones. The source’s Sagyejeol Hair Salon example illustrates describing a real salon; it is not a newly verified client profile.
  • Photos and videos: show the actual exterior, interior and representative menu, products or services with appropriate permission. The original referenced a 27.4% ground-floor figure without enough underlying data to independently reconstruct its categories. Choose entrance photos because they help visitors recognize the place, rather than inferring passing trade from that figure.

2. Make Feedback Easy and Replies Relevant

Reviews provide information about real experiences. Keep both the invitation and the public reply appropriate to that purpose.

  • Replies: address the actual concern and explain only facts, checks or improvements you can support. Avoid private details and an automatic promise of a better next visit.
  • Requests: a review link or QR code on a receipt or near the counter can offer a convenient route for genuine customers. Keep it optional and neutral, without rewards, positive-only selection, pressure or requested wording.

3. Share Real News Through Supported Posts

  • Useful updates: explain a genuine menu change, event, offer or closure with accurate dates and terms. The source’s salon-perm promotion is a hypothetical illustration, not a current offer from a named business. Check the applicable publication status.
  • The next action: use a supported action and relevant destination where available, such as more information or a booking route. Not every profile or post has every button, and a click is different from a completed booking.

A Routine You Can Keep Up With

  • Review changes: the original suggested a monthly post and photo check. Correct important facts when they change; use the check to find omissions rather than create unnecessary updates.
  • Use relevant local language: mention Jochiwon-eup, Jochiwon, Chunghyeon-ro or Saenae-ro where it helps explain the actual business. Repetition and unsupported “best” claims do not answer a customer’s question.
  • Choose useful photographs: prioritize clear, representative images and a correct public address and pin. The checked Google photo and ranking guidance does not establish the original suggested SEO benefit from embedding location coordinates in an image file.
  • Answer common questions: use the channels actually available, including a suitable website page. Do not assume every profile has an editable Q&A section or the former bulk Q&A API, which Google ended on November 3, 2025.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does every Jochiwon business need a profile?

Consider eligibility and where customers discover the business. A profile is useful for maintaining information people see on Search or Maps, but it is not mandatory for every business or a guarantee of being found first.

How do reviews relate to local visibility?

Google says reviews and positive ratings can contribute to local prominence. Invite genuine feedback without incentives or selection for positivity, and reply with relevant facts and appropriate privacy. There is no guaranteed ranking gain simply from answering every review.

What should I do in a larger category such as Korean food?

Explain the actual menu, service and setting so customers can decide whether it fits their plans. Use representative photos and worthwhile updates. The source’s category count does not determine every search result or prove that a particular posting schedule will win more customers.

Start with the Detail That Needs Attention

Check the profile for information that could disrupt a visit: the address, hours, service or contact route. Then agree on who will keep those details current and what work would help a customer choose.
