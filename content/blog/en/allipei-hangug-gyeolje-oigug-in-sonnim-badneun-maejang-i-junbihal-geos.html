When a visitor asks “Alipay?” at the counter, staff need a clear answer. In areas serving customers from China, Southeast Asia or Japan, mobile-wallet acceptance may matter alongside cash and international cards.

For a business owner, “yes” or “no” is only the beginning. Alipay, Alipay+, ZeroPay QR codes and card terminals use different services and connections.

In our work on business profiles, we recommend explaining the options before customers reach checkout. Profile photographs, counter signs and staff instructions should describe the same payment setup.

Alipay and Alipay+ are different

Alipay is a mobile-payment app widely used in mainland China. Alipay+ is a service connecting multiple wallets and banking apps to participating merchants. Its official merchant information describes a unified wallet gateway, with QR and NFC payment products.

Ask which wallets your actual QR code or acquiring provider supports. Acceptance of one app does not establish acceptance of every Alipay+ partner wallet.

  • Alipay: the consumer payment app.
  • Alipay+: a service connecting multiple payment apps and merchant networks.
  • A ZeroPay-linked QR code: one route for supported overseas-wallet payments at participating Korean businesses.
  • A card terminal: a separate acceptance route for supported international cards or prepaid cards.

What should a Korean business check?

The original May 2026 article quoted a Korea Easy Payment Foundation description of an open QR network linked to 37 overseas apps across 20 countries. That is historical network context, not a current acceptance list for your business. The foundation's pages now display differing totals, so a single headline number is not a reliable way to establish which wallet your shop can take.

The foundation's 2023 Alipay+ announcement and later reporting on tourism QR kits describe visitors paying with supported home-country apps. For an owner, the next step is to confirm the actual provider, enrollment and QR route:

  • Is the business enrolled with ZeroPay or another relevant provider?
  • Are overseas-wallet payments enabled on this QR code?
  • Which Alipay or Alipay+ acceptance signs are appropriate for the enabled service?
  • What are the fees and settlement arrangements?
  • Can staff explain the correct payment steps?

Where customers can become confused

A customer may ask about Alipay only when it is time to pay. If staff cannot explain the available option, the customer may assume the purchase cannot go ahead. Typical situations worth preparing for include:

  • The customer shows a wallet app, but the shop only has a card terminal.
  • A QR code is displayed, but staff do not know which overseas wallets it supports.
  • The profile mentions card acceptance but says nothing useful about mobile wallets.
  • Staff ask the customer to scan without explaining any required amount entry.
  • No alternative is offered when a transaction fails.

Clear instructions can resolve some of these problems; others require a provider or terminal change. First establish what works, then explain it consistently.

Keep counter signs short

Choose simple statements that match the payment flow you have tested:

  • Alipay / supported Alipay+ wallets accepted
  • Please scan this QR code to pay.
  • Please enter the payment amount in your app.
  • Please show the payment confirmation screen.
  • If this payment fails, you can pay by card or cash.

Use scanning and manual amount entry only where your provider's flow calls for them. Confirm a successful transaction in the merchant's own records; a customer screenshot alone is not enough. Display only accepted methods and alternatives, and check with the provider before using its branding.

Show payment information in useful photographs

Customers who research the business on Google Maps may look at photographs and reviews before arriving. If you accept Alipay, make the actual checkout guidance visible where appropriate.

  • The Alipay or supported-wallet acceptance sign at the counter.
  • The QR-payment instruction sign.
  • A counter photograph with readable English and Chinese instructions.
  • The relevant menu items or products.
  • A combined sign showing supported card, WOWPASS and eligible tax-refund options.

Useful photographs explain the visit as well as showing the décor. Keep payment screens, customer details and private transaction information out of published images. Wallet acceptance and tax-refund eligibility are separate matters.

Give staff a few consistent phrases

Staff do not need a lengthy English explanation. They need a few accurate phrases for the flow the business supports:

  • Yes, we accept Alipay.
  • Please scan this QR code.
  • Please enter the amount here.
  • Please show us the payment confirmation.
  • This payment did not go through.
  • Do you have another payment method?
  • You can also pay by card or cash.

Chinese-language guidance can also help. Before retrying a transaction or asking for another payment, check whether the first attempt succeeded.

Consider the other wallets your customers use

Visitors arrive with different habits and apps. Some may use Alipay or WeChat Pay; others may ask about PayPay, Touch 'n Go eWallet, TrueMoney or GCash. These are examples, not a rule about every customer's nationality or a promise that every app is enabled.

Alipay+ and ZeroPay are relevant because they connect payment services. Check the specific combinations your provider supports:

  • Visitors using Chinese-language wallets: check Alipay and WeChat Pay separately.
  • Visitors from Japan: check whether a requested app such as PayPay is supported.
  • Visitors from Southeast Asia: confirm the particular wallet, not just the region.
  • Other options: supported international cards, prepaid cards and cash.

Your contract, terminal and QR setup determine actual availability. Test before listing an option on the website or profile.

Common mistakes

  • Displaying an Alipay logo without testing the payment route.
  • Giving different answers about QR acceptance across staff members.
  • Publishing no useful checkout information in profile photographs.
  • Checking only Alipay while overlooking other apps customers actually ask about.
  • Having no explanation of available card or cash alternatives after a failure.
  • Signing up without understanding fees and settlement.

List the methods customers can actually use at your counter. Staff should know what to offer when the requested option is unavailable.

Frequently asked questions

Can a Korean business accept Alipay?

Participating businesses can. Confirm your provider, merchant enrollment and enabled QR or other acceptance route, then test it before displaying an acceptance sign.

Are Alipay and Alipay+ the same?

No. Alipay is a consumer payment app. Alipay+ connects multiple apps to participating merchant networks. Check which specific apps are available through your provider.

Can we show the logo in Google Maps photographs?

Show accurate photographs of the acceptance guidance actually used by your business, following the platform and provider's branding rules. Do not advertise a method that has not been enabled.

What should we say if a payment fails?

After checking the transaction status, say: “This payment did not go through. Do you have another payment method?” Offer only alternatives your business accepts.
