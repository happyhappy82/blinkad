What helps a clinic become easier to identify and understand when patients ask an AI service for recommendations? We would begin with five information checks: responsibility, clear answers, supporting sources, accurate updates and relevant external information.

SOCi's 1.2% figure concerns measured US multi-location businesses, not all hospitals. Its retail overlap and local-search comparison likewise do not establish a universal probability for a Korean clinic.

The five areas below are a practical review framework, not five proven properties shared by every clinic recommended by ChatGPT or Perplexity. The Korean original was published April 23, 2026; the source checks and current-feature notes in this English edition are dated October 31, 2026.

First, make responsibility for clinical information clear

What the evidence supports

Clear authorship and appropriate clinical responsibility help readers assess medical information. They should not be turned into an unsupported claim that Google refuses every medical page without a named physician on every URL.

The original article quoted a marketing guide as if it defined a universal AI eligibility rule. Google's own guidance emphasizes helpful, reliable information and established search practices; it does not publish that particular gate.

How to apply the principle

State who actually wrote the material, who reviewed clinical claims where relevant, and what their role was. Link to an accurate biography or qualification record when useful. Do not invent physician authorship, a review history or a requirement to publish every clinician's licence number.

Person and sameAs markup can describe a real person and genuine identity links. The following is an illustrative outline, not a required AI configuration:

{
  "@type": "Person",
  "name": "[Actual author's name]",
  "jobTitle": "[Actual professional role]",
  "sameAs": []
}

Only add verified profile URLs that refer to the same person. Markup must agree with the visible page. Missing this example does not establish automatic exclusion from AI answers.

Second, bring the useful answer toward the beginning

What the evidence supports

Answering the reader's question early is a useful editorial practice. A rigid “first 60–100 words only” extraction window, or automatic rejection of an answer after word 400, has not been established here.

How to apply the principle

Compare two outlines for a LASIK recovery page. This first version delays the question:

Clinic introduction
General procedure descriptions
Fees
The recovery question finally appears near the end

A more useful outline begins with the question and its essential conditions:

How should a patient plan recovery after LASIK?
Explain early recovery and visual stabilization as different stages.
Use appropriate evidence and the treating clinician's actual guidance.
Describe the relevant assessment, follow-up and warning signs.
Then provide the fuller explanation.

The original example's 5,000 procedures, two-day average and fixed aftercare timetable were not supported by a supplied clinic dataset. They must not be copied as actual results. The FDA notes that visual stabilization can take up to three to six months; individual guidance matters.

Two to five opening sentences may work for a straightforward question. Add the space needed for material risks and conditions rather than treating sentence count as an AI threshold.

Third, support the answer and use markup accurately

What the evidence supports

The original article attributed a 40% citation-weight increase to FAQ schema plus inline references. That specific combined effect was not independently substantiated and is not a Google ranking rule.

Two separate tasks

Useful questions and answers: address genuine patient needs. Google stopped showing FAQ rich results on May 7, 2026; it does not require special AI schema. FAQPage may describe visible content but does not guarantee a citation.

Supporting references: connect a meaningful claim to the relevant study, guideline or official information, with the conditions and date clear. Choose references that help readers assess the answer.

The original snippet's 0.3% complication figure and claimed 1% guideline threshold were not verified and are not used in this example. A complication rate needs its definition, denominator, follow-up and appropriate clinical source.

The source's 78% versus 62% citation-display comparison also lacks a reconstructed methodology here. It should not determine how a clinic writes medical claims.

Fourth, keep changing information accurate and date real revisions

What the research actually measured

  • Ahrefs reported 76.4% recently updated among 564 URLs with detectable dates drawn from its top 1,000 ChatGPT-cited pages. This is not the denominator of the separate 17-million-citation freshness study.
  • The original's “85% outside the organic top ten” figure is a historical study claim, not a universal entry rule. Different studies, dates, query sets and URL-versus-domain definitions can produce different overlaps.
  • The cited 26.6% to 44.4% Overview coverage comparison also describes a particular historical tracking set; it is not the proportion of every patient's search today.

How to apply the principle

Review the pages where an outdated service, price, qualification or instruction could affect a decision. An older page can remain useful; a recent date does not make unsupported claims reliable.

  • Use dateModified for an actual substantive change.
  • Show an accurate revision or information-check date where it helps the reader.
  • Update changing prices, statistics and service details when evidence changes.
  • Add clinical material only with a lawful basis, appropriate evidence and the necessary review.

Reviewing twenty important pages every ninety days can be an internal planning option. It is not evidence that refreshing twenty pages always outperforms writing a hundred, and dates should not be advanced simply to look fresh.

Fifth, check the relevant external information about the clinic

What the hotel research can tell us

Cloudbeds studied 145 consistently top-ranked properties across six destinations. Its reported citation mix included 55.3% online travel agencies, while 72.4% of recommended properties were affiliated with a brand or group. Its public findings also identify official hotel websites as a meaningful source.

External visibility therefore deserves investigation, but hotel results cannot establish a Korean healthcare ranking algorithm.

  • The original's YouTube 98%, travel-blog 97% and Reddit 95% figures were not independently reconstructed from the open primary page. Presence on a platform must not be confused with its share of AI citations.

For a clinic, relevant places to check may include:

  • Korea: accurate Naver Place and KakaoMap information, plus applicable medical platforms whose terms and legal arrangements fit the clinic.
  • International patients: the actual Google Business Profile and appropriate official or professional listings. US-specific sites such as Healthgrades or WebMD are not automatically registration routes for every Korean clinic.

Use the analogy carefully

Find out which sources actually appear for the clinic's relevant questions, then check whether they describe the right institution accurately. Neither a particular app listing nor fifty or a hundred reviews is a proven universal prerequisite. A 4.3-star average should not become a mandatory cutoff.

Practical listing checks

  • Is the eligible Google Business Profile accurate and properly managed, with genuine feedback handled appropriately?
  • Does Naver Place show current information, and what actually appears for relevant searches?
  • Are any specialty-platform listings relevant, accurate and consistent with the clinic's arrangements?
  • Are KakaoMap and other useful local details consistent with the website?

Use the checklist to identify what needs follow-up. Three completed areas do not prove inclusion, and fewer than four do not establish exclusion.

Use the five areas to improve information, then observe the result

These checks help organize a useful clinic-content review. There is no verified formula in which AI multiplies five signals and discards a clinic whenever one is weak.

The work still needs appropriate medical-advertising assessment. Physician videos, internal figures and patient stories do not become automatically permissible because they avoid words such as “best.”

Start with three questions this week:

  • Do important pages answer the reader's question near the beginning, without dropping essential conditions?
  • Do relevant profiles and genuine reviews help the reader verify the clinic's actual information?
  • Do the ten most useful pages contain any facts that need correction or substantive updating?

Use the findings alongside actual acquisition data before reallocating advertising spend. Measure the return using the clinic's actual results.
