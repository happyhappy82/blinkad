When choosing a hospital marketing agency, owners often compare portfolios and prices first. Disappointment months later may come from a different issue: a report shows more impressions and clicks, but the clinic sees little corresponding growth in appointments. The problem can be an undefined division of responsibility, rather than skill alone. Here is what to clarify before signing.

The agency's responsibility may cover only part of the process

Hospital marketing extends from visibility to return visits:

Visibility → Inquiry → Consultation → Booking → Visit → Return visit

Ad-management contracts often cover the first stage and the beginning of the second: generating exposure and inquiries. Later stages may remain the clinic's responsibility.

If the boundary is unclear, the clinic may think it has outsourced marketing while the agency believes it is managing advertising. More inquiries without more bookings then frustrate both.

Start with one question:

“Who reviews what happens after an inquiry arrives?”

Three things to look for in the report

1. Are there figures beyond impressions and clicks?

Agency reports commonly show impressions, clicks and rankings. To understand the link to revenue, ask at least how many inquiries arrived and how many became bookings. If nobody can answer, that stage may not be monitored.

2. Can you identify the inquiry channel?

Inquiries may arrive by phone, KakaoTalk, Naver TalkTalk or Instagram DM. If you cannot identify where advertising inquiries arrive, it is difficult to tell which ads contributed.

3. Can the clinic check the numbers?

The clinic should be able to inspect the figures in its own accounts and records. Look for verifiable metrics such as profile views and call-related records, and check exactly what each measure counts.

Five questions before signing

1. Who owns our accounts?

If the ad account, Business Profile or website domain is held by the agency, recovering access and materials at the end can be difficult. We recommend clinic ownership from the outset, with management access granted to the agency.

2. What remains with the clinic when the contract ends?

Traffic from paid ads depends on keeping those ads running. Profile information, content and consultation records can remain useful after a campaign. Establish what the clinic will retain.

3. Who replies to inquiries?

Some agencies also handle consultations. If so, check where conversation records are stored and whether the clinic can access them.

4. How are international inquiries handled?

Decide this in advance for a clinic serving international patients or operating in a tourist area. If the agency manages only Korean-language channels, somebody still needs responsibility for other-language inquiries.

5. What changes if results are weak?

Look for an answer that starts by finding where progress stops, rather than immediately increasing spend. It reveals whether the agency reviews the wider process or only the ad campaign.

When several companies share the work

A clinic may use different companies for clinical records software, advertising, promotional visits and international-patient recruitment, with separate contracts and reporting formats.

That arrangement can work. The handover between companies is where responsibilities may be missed. An agency may generate inquiries while responsibility for replying and continuing the consultation falls outside its contract.

Start investigating missed handovers at any stage without an assigned owner.

Frequently asked questions

Why do agency prices vary so much?

Sometimes prices differ for similar work, but often the included stages differ. Align the scope before comparing the amount.

What is a suitable contract length?

Search and content take time to develop, so a very short period can be difficult to assess. For a longer contract, agree on interim review dates and criteria beforehand.

Does changing agencies mean starting again?

Clinic-owned accounts and materials make continuity possible. That is why ownership is the first question.

Should we do it ourselves or outsource?

Creating visibility takes ongoing work and can be efficient to outsource. Inquiry handling and consultations need to leave records the clinic controls, including when another company helps. Consider those responsibilities separately.

Before choosing an agency

Check these points alongside the portfolio:

  • Is the scope of responsibility written into the contract?
  • Does the report include inquiries as well as impressions and clicks?
  • Does the clinic own its accounts and materials?
  • How will inquiries in other languages be handled?
  • What will change if results are weak?
