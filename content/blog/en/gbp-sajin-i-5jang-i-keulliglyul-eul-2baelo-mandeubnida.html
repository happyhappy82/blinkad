Customers use photographs on Google to answer practical questions: where is the entrance, what is the space like, and what products or services can they choose? Before adding more images, we recommend asking what information customers still need to decide whether to visit.

The Google guidance checked for this update does not substantiate a twofold increase in click-through rate from five photos. The practical aim here is to provide images customers need to understand the business, then assess the results using actual records.

We will look at five photo types: exterior, interior, representative products, work in progress and the team. The appropriate number depends on the business and its space. Google’s business-specific photo guidance likewise covers different types of images.

What can Business Profile photos help customers understand?

Photographs show customers the actual business. Their number or category alone does not establish how much click-through rate, inquiries or revenue will change. Results observed for other businesses or periods should not become a promised result for your store.

To assess a change, record the images updated and the date, then examine matching periods for profile interactions and actual inquiries. Separate call-button and website clicks in Business Profile performance from confirmed bookings and visits.

Five photo types to prepare first

1. Exterior: can customers find the entrance?

Show the sign and entrance together. Consider the direction customers approach from, particularly where a business is harder to find after dark or from a side street.

For an upstairs business, separate photos of the building and the business entrance can help. Check that the sign and route reflect current access.

2. Interior: can customers see the space they will use?

Show seating, circulation and the atmosphere from the customer’s perspective. Photograph a tidy, well-lit space without editing it to appear larger or substantially different.

Where table spacing or particular facilities matter, include images that make those features understandable. Check that the spaces shown still serve the same purpose.

3. Products or menu items: what can customers choose?

Photograph items that are actually sold, showing the appearance and contents customers need to make a choice. Check whether seasonal or discontinued products remain prominent in the images.

Compare menu names with the current menu too. Where international customers need translated guidance, check the images and descriptions together.

4. Work and services: what does the business provide?

Show genuine services or production processes, such as the working area of a salon or preparation of a café drink. Choose scenes that help customers understand the service.

Check the permitted scope of photographing and publishing identifiable people. For clinics and other businesses with additional advertising requirements, examine what the image shows and the accompanying explanation together.

5. Team or owner: introduce the people customers will meet

Use natural images of the team that actually operates the business. Choose scenes that help customers understand the service rather than imposing the same pose on every type of business.

Update images when the team or uniforms change so that the public information still reflects the business.

Review images that could confuse customers

Google’s photo guidance emphasizes accurate representation, focus, lighting and avoiding excessive alteration. Check whether an image:

  • Is too blurred to identify the product or entrance.
  • Uses filters or editing that substantially change the actual appearance.
  • Makes a different place or unavailable product look like part of the business.
  • Shows an old interior or operating information that is no longer accurate.

Owners’ images and customers’ images have different management procedures. Check policy violations and the applicable management and reporting process, rather than assuming an owner can delete a customer’s photograph simply because they dislike it.

Uploading photos and checking specifications

Select the profile you manage in Google Search or Maps and follow the current photo-upload instructions. Check the public profile afterward too.

The profile must be verified for photos to appear publicly. Use the photo and video management guidance to distinguish Pending, Not approved and Live uploads. Pending photos are not visible to customers; Google says photos can take 24–48 hours to appear on the profile.

Google’s photo guidance gives these specifications:

Item Official guidance
Format JPG or PNG
File size 10 KB–5 MB
Recommended resolution 720 × 720 pixels
Minimum resolution 250 × 250 pixels
Quality In focus, appropriately lit and representative of reality

Confirm that the images have uploaded and are visible. Review them when the interior, menu or services change instead of treating an upload schedule as a guaranteed ranking improvement.

Frequently asked questions

Is a smartphone suitable?

Focus, lighting and a recognizable representation of the actual place matter more than the equipment’s name. Clean the lens, keep the camera steady and inspect the image at its intended public size.

Are five photos enough?

This article groups the information into five types. Google recommends multiple photos for some categories, including exterior and interior images. Choose enough to explain the actual space and services.

What if a customer’s image appears first?

Google does not guarantee that the owner’s selected image will always appear first. Prepare useful, current images and use the official process to report material that violates the policy.
