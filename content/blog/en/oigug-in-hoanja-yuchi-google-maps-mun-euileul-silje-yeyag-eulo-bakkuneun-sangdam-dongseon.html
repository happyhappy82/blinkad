Responding to an inquiry in another language takes more than translating the sentences. Staff need to check that they have understood the requested date and cost information correctly. At a shift change, the handover needs to identify any unanswered questions.

In DoctorNest, staff can view the original inquiry, its translation and earlier conversations together. They compare the messages before replying and leave a note about what the next colleague still needs to check.

Ask about handling international patient inquiries

Read the original message alongside the translation

If a translated date or treatment area is unclear, return to the original message. Check whether the patient asked about several procedures together and whether they provided one preferred date or several. Do not enter an interpretation into the booking record without confirming it.

Seeing the original and translation together in DoctorNest helps staff revisit the conversation and ask for clarification. If the exchange has become lengthy, answer the patient's actual questions first. Use a short follow-up question to clarify anything uncertain.

The languages and channels used for international inquiries vary by clinic. During the adoption discussion, check that the required accounts and channels can be connected and that staff can read the original messages and translations on their PCs.

Keep recurring information short and clear

Confirmed information, such as the clinic's location, opening hours and appointment inquiry process, can be prepared as saved replies. Explain what the patient needs to know first, rather than packing several conditions into one sentence. Include the relevant conditions whenever you give a date or amount.

For inquiries outside staffed hours, state when the clinic can realistically resume the conversation. After an initial reply, the next staff member still needs to check whether the patient's question has been answered.

Receiving a preferred visit date does not mean an appointment is confirmed. The booking team needs to verify availability. Questions about whether a procedure is suitable, or other clinical decisions, need to go to the medical team. Agree on which routine information staff can provide and which questions require further confirmation.

Record what the next colleague needs to answer

Copying only the translation into a handover leaves the next person searching for what has already been explained. Check the replies in DoctorNest's conversation history, then note the questions that still need an answer.

For example, a patient may ask about both cost and available dates, but receive an answer only about dates. Record who will check the price and its conditions. There is no need to send an estimated amount that has not been verified.

If the original and translation seem to say different things, mark that point in the note so the next colleague can clarify it first. If the discussion needs an interpreter or a clinician's explanation, assign someone at the clinic to arrange the next step.

Tell us which languages and questions you handle

For an initial discussion, tell us which channels and languages your inquiries come through and which questions staff find difficult to answer. You do not need to send real patient conversations or personal information in advance.
