If your business serves visitors from China, you may have wondered whether to accept WeChat Pay. In areas such as Myeongdong, Hongdae, Seongsu, Apgujeong, Jeju and Busan, knowing how they can pay may help customers decide whether to enter a shop.

For an owner, the practical question involves more than the logo. WeChat Pay, Weixin Pay, ZeroPay QR codes, cross-border wallet connections and card terminals are related parts of the payment experience, but they are not interchangeable.

In our work reviewing business profiles, we encourage owners to connect the information customers see before visiting with the guidance they receive at the counter. Staff should know which methods work and how to offer an available alternative if a payment fails.

Understand the WeChat Pay and Weixin Pay names

Customers may use “WeChat Pay” internationally or “Weixin Pay” in the mainland Chinese ecosystem. TenPay Global describes itself as Tencent's cross-border payment platform, connecting the Weixin ecosystem, payment partners and international users.

Before putting up a sign, identify the actual service through which your business can accept a visitor's payment.

  • WeChat Pay: a name customers may recognize internationally.
  • Weixin Pay: the name commonly used in the mainland Chinese ecosystem.
  • TenPay Global: Tencent's cross-border payment platform.
  • ZeroPay QR: one route through which participating Korean merchants can accept supported overseas wallets.

How can a Korean business accept these payments?

The original May 2026 article cited a ZeroPay network description covering 20 countries and 67 overseas payment apps. Those were network-level figures, not a list of wallets enabled at every shop. The Korea Easy Payment Foundation also described links with Alipay, WeChat Pay and UnionPay in its earlier announcement.

Network descriptions change, and the foundation's current pages display differing totals. For your business, confirm the supported wallet and payment route with your provider, then test the actual setup.

  • Is the business enrolled with ZeroPay or another relevant provider?
  • Is overseas-wallet acceptance enabled?
  • Has the intended WeChat Pay or Weixin Pay route been tested?
  • Can staff verify the completed transaction in the merchant's own payment records?
  • Have you checked the fees and settlement arrangements?

Keep it distinct from Alipay

Alipay and WeChat Pay may both be familiar to visitors from China. You can consider both, but staff need to recognize which service the customer is asking about.

A customer might say “WeChat Pay,” show the app or point to 微信支付. Staff do not need a long technical explanation; they need to know which payment route the shop actually supports.

  • Alipay: confirm acceptance of that app through your provider; Alipay+ is a separate multi-wallet service.
  • WeChat Pay / Weixin Pay: confirm the supported Weixin wallet-payment route.
  • UnionPay: check app-based QR payments and card acceptance separately.
  • International cards: confirm which Visa, Mastercard or other cards your terminal accepts.

Prepare short signs at the counter

A customer checking out needs a quick answer. Choose only the statements that match your verified setup:

  • WeChat Pay accepted
  • Weixin Pay accepted
  • Please scan this QR code to pay.
  • Please enter the amount in your app.
  • Please show the payment confirmation screen.
  • If this payment fails, you can pay by card or cash.

Use the scanning and amount-entry instructions only for a flow that actually requires them. A customer's confirmation screen can help resolve a question, but staff should confirm the transaction in the merchant system before treating it as paid. If many customers read Chinese, you can also display:

  • 支持微信支付 — WeChat Pay accepted.
  • 请扫码支付 — Please scan the QR code to pay.
  • 请出示付款成功页面 — Please show the successful-payment screen.

Test acceptance before using an “accepted” sign. Offer card or cash as alternatives only where the business can actually take them.

Show useful payment information in Google Maps photographs

Some visitors consult Google Maps, other map services, social media and reviews before choosing a business. A clear photograph can help them understand how checkout works before arriving.

  • The verified WeChat Pay or Weixin Pay sign at the counter.
  • The QR-payment instruction sign.
  • A counter view with readable English and Chinese guidance.
  • The relevant products or menu items.
  • A combined sign for the cards, WOWPASS, Alipay or tax-refund service you actually support.

Include practical information alongside photographs of the space. Keep customer payment screens, transaction details and personal information out of published images. A tax-refund service has separate eligibility conditions; accepting a payment wallet does not make a business tax-refund eligible.

Agree on a few staff phrases

Even a simple QR transaction can become confusing if nobody can explain the next step. Prepare short phrases and match them to your provider's process:

  • Yes, we accept WeChat Pay.
  • Please scan this QR code.
  • Please enter the amount here.
  • Please show us the payment confirmation.
  • The payment has not been completed yet.
  • Do you have another payment method?
  • You can also pay by card or cash.

Staff can point to a Chinese-language sign if they are not comfortable saying the phrase. Before retrying a payment, check its status to avoid an unnecessary duplicate charge.

Review payment options together

A visitor may ask about Alipay, WeChat Pay, UnionPay, an international card, cash or a tax-refund receipt. Organizing the answers together can make checkout easier.

One clear sign is often more useful than several conflicting notices. Include only the supported entries:

  • Alipay or supported Alipay+ partner wallets.
  • WeChat Pay / Weixin Pay.
  • UnionPay, specifying card or QR acceptance where needed.
  • Visa / Mastercard.
  • WOWPASS.
  • Tax Free or Tax Refund information for eligible purchases at an eligible store.

A short, accurate list is easier for customers to use. A payment network's general coverage does not confirm that every wallet or feature works at your counter.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Displaying a WeChat Pay logo without testing acceptance.
  • Describing Alipay and WeChat Pay as the same service.
  • Failing to verify that a QR transaction succeeded in the merchant system.
  • Providing no useful payment information in profile photographs.
  • Giving different answers depending on which staff member is asked.
  • Having no explanation of available alternatives after a failed payment.

Payment difficulties can become part of a customer's review, even when they enjoyed the food or product. Clear explanations help staff deal with the problem as it happens.

A WeChat Pay readiness checklist

  • We have checked our ZeroPay or other overseas-QR connection.
  • We have tested the intended WeChat Pay / Weixin Pay payment route.
  • Staff understand the scanning, amount-entry and merchant-confirmation steps that apply.
  • We have a phrase for offering an available alternative when payment fails.
  • English or Chinese guidance is visible at the counter.
  • Our Google Maps photographs show useful payment guidance.
  • We respond appropriately when a review raises a payment question.
  • We understand the fees and settlement process.

Respond to payment questions in reviews

If a review mentions WeChat Pay, QR payment or a failed transaction, acknowledge the relevant point. Other customers may read the reply when planning their visit. For example:

  • Thank you for visiting. We're glad paying by QR code was convenient.
  • Thank you for your review. Our staff can explain the WeChat Pay process before you pay.
  • We're sorry checkout was confusing. We'll review our payment instructions with the team.

Use a response that reflects what happened and what the team will actually do. Resolve individual transaction details privately rather than posting payment or personal information in a public reply.

Frequently asked questions

Can a Korean business accept WeChat Pay?

Participating businesses can. Availability depends on the provider, merchant enrollment and enabled payment route. Confirm those details and test the setup before advertising acceptance.

Are WeChat Pay and Weixin Pay different?

The names are used in different language and market contexts. For a Korean merchant, recognize the names customers use, then confirm the supported wallet and cross-border route with the provider. A familiar name alone does not establish account eligibility or acceptance.

Can we show the logo in Google Maps photographs?

You can show an accurate photograph of your actual payment guidance, subject to the platform and provider's branding rules. Display only services the business supports.

What should staff say when a payment fails?

After checking the transaction status, a useful phrase is: “The payment has not been completed. Do you have another payment method?” Explain the card or cash alternatives your business can accept.
