One question comes up often in our consultations: “Would international customers really come to a business like ours?”

Our answer depends on the business. Some are well suited to this audience; others face practical difficulties. Here is how we assess the fit.

The industry name alone does not decide the answer

Owners often begin with the category: restaurants could work, while a tutoring business or repair garage probably would not.

Yet businesses in the same category can have very different customer mixes. Imagine two similar restaurants on the same street: international guests could make up half the customers at one and be absent from the other. The category alone cannot explain that difference.

We look at four conditions.

Four conditions make a business a better fit

First, can customers buy and use the service during their stay in Korea?

The original article cites approximately 10.71 million international arrivals in the first half of 2026 (Korean), up 21.3% from the same period a year earlier. Visitors eat, shop and use beauty or medical services during their stay. If the purchase can be completed at your business within that time, it meets this condition.

Second, is it something people look for on a map?

A visitor may not know a business name. They can open a map and search for the type of business nearby. Restaurants, cafés, salons, nail shops, pharmacies, clinics, massage businesses and currency exchanges fit that discovery route. A business that gets most work through referrals or contracts may have fewer opportunities to meet customers through Maps.

Third, how much conversation is needed?

When the process is mainly choosing, ordering and paying, the language barrier can be lower. Photographs and a price list may answer many of the initial questions. A service requiring a 30-minute consultation needs considerably more preparation, including the ability to handle the conversation.

Owners also ask which language to prepare first. KTO's 2025 figures show China, Japan, Taiwan and the United States as the largest inbound markets, in that order. The actual customer mix varies by area, though. We first examine the languages already appearing in the business's Google reviews to understand the customers who have used it, rather than choosing from national totals alone.

Fourth, is the location within reach of international customers?

Proximity to visitor routes or neighborhoods with international residents helps, but do not define the area too narrowly. In the businesses we review, we find that customers may be willing to travel two or three subway stops for a suitable place.

When the fit is more difficult

Some situations call for a more cautious recommendation.

Services requiring detailed Korean-language consultation. If staff must work through documents or hold ongoing aftercare conversations, attracting a customer will not resolve the communication problem. Prepare the response process first.

Services whose value depends on repeated visits. A ten-visit program is not a natural match for someone staying three days. A different audience may be more appropriate.

Categories visitors have little reason to seek out in Korea. If they can get the same thing more cheaply at home or have no reason to buy it during a trip, more exposure cannot create the missing reason to search.

International residents may be a better audience than tourists

This is where the assessment can change.

Korea's international population includes people who live here as well as visitors. Ministry of Justice figures put foreign nationals staying in Korea at about 2.65 million at the end of 2024, up 5.2% from 2023 and a record at that time. That total includes both long- and short-term stays; it is not a count of long-term residents alone. Students, workers, expatriate staff and marriage migrants living in Korea have different needs from short-stay visitors. They can use clinics and dentists, become regular salon customers, attend classes and have vehicles repaired over a longer period.

A service needing repeated visits may fit residents particularly well. They can return.

Before concluding that an industry is unsuitable, we reconsider the audience.

Check where your business stands

How many of these seven statements apply?

  • Customers complete their purchase at the business.
  • People could search for the business category on Maps.
  • Photographs and prices explain much of the offer.
  • Visitor routes or international neighborhoods are accessible by public transport.
  • Card or convenient electronic payment is available.
  • There is at least one channel for reservations or inquiries.
  • The business is listed on Google Maps.

Five or more suggests a reasonable starting point. With three or four, identify the preparations first. With two or fewer, address the service and customer experience first, before trying to attract more people.
