“I’m never going back.” A review like that can be difficult to read after a long day running a business. The next decision matters: a hurried, hostile reply can create a second problem in full view of future customers.

This article looks at three reply styles worth avoiding, then offers calmer ways to respond and the current Google process for reporting a possible policy violation. A negative review is not automatically malicious, false or removable.

Write for the Next Customer Too

If a review feels unfair, pause before answering. Give yourself time to read the concern and check what you actually know.

Your response is public. It gives the reviewer context, but it also shows someone considering a first visit how the business handles disagreement. You cannot know exactly how many people will read it or how each person will react.

The original article linked to a rolling BrightLocal survey page for a figure of 89%. The matching finding appears in its October 2018 study of 1,000 US consumers: respondents read local businesses’ replies. Its methodology excludes non-review-readers in relevant analyses. Treat this as historical survey context, not the share of current Korean customers reading every reply. The newer survey’s similarly numbered questions can measure something different.

Write for both the person who left the review and the prospective customer reading it later. A useful reply explains the business’s response without starting an argument.

Three Reply Styles to Avoid

The following are illustrative examples of unhelpful replies, not quotations from an identified customer dispute.

1. An Angry Counterattack

“You arrived 30 minutes late, so of course the food was cold. How is that our fault? Don’t come back.”

Even if a timing issue is relevant, this wording attacks the customer. Explain a verified service limitation calmly if it helps, and keep the discussion focused on what can be done.

2. Revealing Private Visit Details

“You were here on Tuesday at two, at table two. We can check the CCTV.”

Publishing details to identify or intimidate a reviewer can make other readers uneasy and can expose private information. Do not use surveillance footage or customer records as a threat in a public thread. Handle necessary investigation through an appropriate private process.

3. Mockery or Dismissal

“If our food isn’t good enough for you, try somewhere else. Haha.”

Sarcasm can be read as contempt, even if you intended it as a light remark. A screenshot may circulate beyond the original thread, so choose language you would be comfortable explaining publicly.

Reply styleExampleWhat a reader may take from it
Counterattack“Don’t come back.”The business responds aggressively to criticism.
Private details or pressure“We can check the CCTV.”Customer information may be used to intimidate someone.
Mockery“Try somewhere else. Haha.”The business dismisses a customer’s concern.
You can edit your own reply, but you cannot count on undoing copies shared elsewhere. The original ten-minute pause is a practical habit, not a Google requirement; take the time needed to check the facts and respond appropriately.

Three Calmer Reply Templates

Use these as starting points. Replace brackets with true, relevant information and do not claim that an investigation or improvement has already happened unless it has.

Template 1: Acknowledge a Concern and Explain the Next Step

“Hello, this is [name/role] at [business]. We’re sorry about [the issue you can appropriately acknowledge]. [Briefly state the check or action actually being taken.] If you would like to discuss the details, please contact us at [business phone/email] so we can follow up privately.”

Why it works: it acknowledges the concern, explains a real next step and gives an appropriate private contact. Avoid promising that every problem will certainly be resolved.

Template 2: When the Facts Need Clarification

“Thank you for raising this. We’re sorry the visit was frustrating. We would like to understand [the specific issue] more clearly. Please contact [business contact] if you are comfortable discussing the details with us.”

Why it works: it opens a route to clarify the issue without publishing personal details or presenting unverified assumptions as facts.

Template 3: When You Cannot Match the Account to Your Records

“Thank you for the feedback. We have checked [only the records actually checked] and have not yet been able to match the experience described. Please contact [business contact] if you would like us to look into the details.”

Why it works: it states the limit of what you can establish. An unmatched name or visit record does not prove the reviewer is lying, so do not imply certainty that the review is fabricated.

How to Report a Review That May Violate Google Policy

Reporting is appropriate when the content appears to violate a policy, such as spam, harassment, private information or a conflict of interest. Disagreement with a negative opinion is not, on its own, a reason for removal.

Step 1: sign into the account authorized to manage the correct Business Profile.

Step 2: find the review through the profile or the Reviews Management Tool.

Step 3: use the report option for that review.

Step 4: select the policy reason that accurately fits the content.

Step 5: follow the available submission instructions and provide relevant evidence only through the proper channel. Do not publish private customer records to make your case.

Check the report status instead of repeatedly reporting the same review. The original estimate of a few days to two weeks is not a guaranteed removal deadline. Google’s current process provides a one-time appeal for eligible reviews when a report finds no policy violation; follow the available route and decision.
What if the review is not removed?

A review expressing a genuine negative opinion can remain even when the business disagrees. Use a calm, accurate response where it helps readers. If you believe a policy violation was missed, use the eligible appeal process rather than assuming repeated reports will force removal.

Can a reviewer’s profile prove that they are a competitor?

Public contribution patterns may provide context for a report, but several low ratings in one industry do not prove who operates the account or that every review is false. Do not identify, threaten or publicly accuse a person based on that inference. Submit only relevant, accurate material for Google to assess.

What Review Management Can and Cannot Tell You About Ranking

Google explains local ranking through relevance, distance and prominence. It says review volume and positive ratings can help. That does not establish a fixed ranking bonus for each reply, a required pace of new reviews or a formula based on words customers include.

Replying thoughtfully is worthwhile customer service. Measure search changes and actual inquiries separately, rather than attributing every change to the number of replies.

Set aside a regular time to read reviews, such as ten minutes on a chosen day. Adjust the time to the issue and volume. The routine helps you avoid overlooking feedback, without guaranteeing a visible ranking change.

Aim for a Useful Response, Not a Public Victory

Criticism is part of operating a business. A careful reply can give readers information about how you handle concerns, though it cannot guarantee that every dispute will improve the business’s reputation.

Before you publish a reply, check these five points:

  • Pause before replying. Ten minutes is a starting suggestion; take longer if you need to check the facts.
  • Avoid counterattacks, private details and mockery.
  • Adapt the templates to the real concern and only state actions or facts you can support.
  • Report apparent policy violations through Google’s process, without assuming an unwelcome review is false.
  • Read feedback consistently and address useful operational lessons; do not promise that replies automatically raise rankings.
