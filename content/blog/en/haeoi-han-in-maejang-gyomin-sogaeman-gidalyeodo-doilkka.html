At a glance
For many Korean-owned businesses outside Korea, the first customers arrive through community referrals. But referrals alone can leave the business out of reach of local customers and newcomers who do not know it yet. Keep those relationships while making the business discoverable through Google Search and Maps.

When opening a business abroad, the first customers often come through people you know. A satisfied customer brings a friend, the business name appears in a Korean community group chat or local forum, and regulars begin to return.

A referred customer has already heard about the business from someone they know, which can provide more initial trust than an advertisement. The difficulty is that when referrals slow down, new customers can slow down with them.

Someone without a personal recommendation may open Google Search or Maps to find a nearby restaurant, salon, dentist or grocery store. If the business is hard to find, or its hours and services are unclear, that customer may choose somewhere else.

Community referrals matter, but a business cannot control their timing

A customer arriving through a recommendation may not need a long explanation of prices and services from the beginning. That is one reason the Korean community remains important to Korean-owned businesses abroad.

However, the business cannot decide when a recommendation spreads, who receives it or how far it travels. Regular customers may pass on old information. Inquiries may change suddenly when community routines shift during moves or holiday periods. Someone who knows nobody who can recommend the business has no such introduction.

The aim is to make search another way to find the business while keeping referrals. Customers who know only the area and business type should be able to discover it too.

New customers search by area and business type before they know your name

Existing customers search for the business name. New customers combine a place and business type—“Korean hair salon Toronto” or “Korean restaurant Singapore,” for example—or look for nearby services in the local language.

Google shows Maps and local results for searches for nearby businesses. Ranking takes account of factors including relevance to the query, distance from the searcher and prominence. Even a business with many Korean customers needs to look beyond Korean searches. Second-generation Koreans, other local residents, international students and visitors may use different expressions. Make the essentials easy to find on Google Maps

Before visiting, someone who finds the business in search needs to check the practical details. Five things should be easy to find:

  • The products or services offered
  • The current address and opening hours
  • Photographs of the exterior, interior, menu or services
  • Recent reviews and replies from the business
  • A next step, such as calling, visiting the website or booking

Google Business Profile lets you manage the address, opening hours, contact details, website and photographs. Google: editing business information Restaurants can add menus, and some business types can connect booking or ordering links. Exterior photographs help first-time customers recognize the place. Accuracy matters more than adding information for its own sake. Old hours after a change in closing days, or a phone number that no longer connects, create difficulties before a customer even visits.

Start local-language information with the questions customers actually ask

It is easy to assume that attracting local customers requires a large multilingual website. Translating everything at once adds cost and makes later updates harder.

Start with one page answering the questions people need resolved before booking or visiting:

BusinessInformation to show first
RestaurantRepresentative dishes, menu photographs, location, hours and how to book
Hair salonAvailable services, portfolio, estimated duration, and consultation and booking routes
Dental clinicAreas of care, clinicians, location and information to prepare before booking
Grocery storeMain product categories, hours, parking and delivery availability, and directions
Legal servicesConsultation areas, supported languages, inquiry process and how to book

Actual services differ from business to business. Do not list unverified services or language support. Use short, clear expressions local customers recognize rather than publishing machine translation unchanged. Restaurants and cafés can also consider how much of a menu to prepare in English, Japanese and Chinese.

Correct business information before starting ads

Google Ads cannot fill in an incomplete profile. Customers who click an ad and find outdated hours, too few photographs or unclear service information may leave without getting in touch.

Before launching, check which searches led to the profile, how many views it received and what actions followed, such as calls, direction requests and website clicks. Google provides performance data for verified profiles, though the available metrics vary by business and account. Google Business Profile: understanding performance

That gives you something to compare after advertising begins. If views rise but inquiries do not, check the page and contact route before adding budget. If calls and direction requests rise but actual attendance is unknown, the business also needs to record how visitors found it.

Help customers find you without a personal introduction

Keep looking after community referrals. At the same time, make it possible for someone encountering the business for the first time to check the details and contact you. That makes the flow of new customers less dependent on introductions alone.

Frequently asked questions

Do we need local-language information if most customers are Korean?

Yes, if you want to welcome other local customers or second-generation Koreans too. You do not need to translate everything. Start with the business type, main services, location, hours and booking instructions people need before visiting.

Should we work on the website or Google Business Profile first?

If the profile is missing or inaccurate, start there. Use a website or language-specific information page for menus, services and consultation conditions that cannot be explained adequately in the profile.

Will updating the profile put us at the top of Maps immediately?

Top placement cannot be guaranteed. Local results depend on factors including relevance, distance and prominence. After preparing accurate information, photographs, reviews and contact routes, keep checking search terms and customer actions.
