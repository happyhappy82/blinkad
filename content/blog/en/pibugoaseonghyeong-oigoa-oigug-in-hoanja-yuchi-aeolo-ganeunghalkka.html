If someone asks Perplexity, “Best dermatology clinic in Seoul for foreigners,” will your clinic appear in the answer?

Korea reported 1,170,467 international patients in 2024, including about 705,000 dermatology patients in the specialty statistics. Japan, China, the United States and Taiwan accounted for 75.8% of the reported nationality shares. These figures establish the scale of the 2024 market; they do not establish which search tool patients used first.

This five-step guide is for clinics registered to attract international patients, or preparing the appropriate arrangements. It explains how useful English information can support discovery through AI search while keeping registration, medical advertising and patient consent in view.

The Korean article was first published on April 27, 2026. This English edition checks sources and legal provisions as of October 31, 2026. The patient figures below remain historical 2024 data, rather than the latest annual total.

Government reporting also distinguishes the approximately KRW 7.5 trillion spent by patients and companions from recorded treatment-card payments.

Countries and territories of origin

  • Japan: approximately 441,000 patients, or 37.7%.
  • China: approximately 260,000, or 22.3%.
  • United States: approximately 101,000, or 8.7%.
  • Taiwan: approximately 83,000, or 7.1%.

The Korean article also cited contemporary reporting of dermatology growth of 1,017% for Taiwan, 278.8% for China and 155.2% for Japan. Those are historical reported comparisons. The underlying earlier-year specialty figures have not been independently reconstructed here, so they should not determine a clinic's current country priorities on their own.

The government's earlier target

The government's May 2023 strategy set a target of 700,000 international patients annually by 2027. The reported 2024 total exceeded that target three years early.

For a clinic, the useful question is whether prospective patients can find clear information about its actual services in a language they understand. The national figures do not demonstrate that English AI answers are an empty market or that a particular clinic will attract those patients.

2. Why an English AI answer might overlook a Korean clinic

We begin by checking the information available about the clinic, rather than assuming that a missing mention has one cause.

Reason 1: limited original English information

A translated company introduction offers less help with a treatment question than a useful English procedure guide. A visitor comparing a treatment may need to understand suitability, assessment, recovery, risks and practical arrangements.

The original article referred to research on English education and medical-tourism communication, including a 2020 study of nursing students. A student sample should not be treated as a measurement of every Korean clinic's language capability. Check the clinic's actual interpreter and communication arrangements.

Reason 2: incomplete information on relevant external sites

Depending on the query, search results and AI answers may refer to official information or commercial platforms such as:

  • Medical Korea, operated by KHIDI, and VisitKorea, operated by the Korea Tourism Organization; these are separate services.
  • MedicalTourism.com and its destination information.
  • Better by MTA.
  • PlacidWay.
  • Services such as K-MedicalTours or Seoul Guide Medical.
  • Relevant travel or wellness information on Tripadvisor.

This is a list of places to investigate, not a measured ranking of AI citation sources. A clinic does not have to appear in every directory to be eligible for an AI answer. Verify a platform's relevance, current terms and any commercial relationship before applying.

Reason 3: unclear or inconsistent page information

Accurate structured data can describe visible information, but it cannot replace the English explanation itself. Google does not require a special AI schema. In particular, a missing FAQPage or MedicalBusiness label does not establish why an AI service omitted a clinic.

Google stopped displaying FAQ rich results on May 7, 2026, and removed that feature's documentation in June. Useful patient FAQs still have a role; FAQ markup should not be sold as a current Google AI visibility requirement.

Reason 4: misunderstanding new health-search products

The original article highlighted the March 19, 2026 Perplexity Health announcement and quoted connections to 1.7 million providers. Partner b.well's announcement currently describes its network as covering more than 2.4 million providers. These are differing source figures about a health-data network, not a count of Korean clinics available for referrals.

A personal health-data connection is different from a directory of Korean clinics. It does not establish a registration route, ranking rule or referral opportunity for a Korean provider. Monitor relevant public search answers and check product eligibility separately; never upload patient records merely to test marketing visibility.

3. A five-step English AEO plan for international patient information

Step 1: prepare treatment-specific English guides — weeks 1–3

These are example planning queries, not newly measured search volumes or endorsements:

- Best clinic for botox in Seoul for foreigners
- How much does filler cost in Korea
- Best dermatology clinic in Gangnam English speaking
- LASIK surgery Korea recommended hospital
- Korean skin booster treatment guide for tourists

The original plan suggests an English guide of roughly 2,000 characters per priority procedure. Treat that as a drafting starting point, rather than a search-engine threshold. Cover what the reader needs:

  • What the procedure is.
  • Which suitability factors a clinician assesses.
  • What the procedure involves.
  • Recovery expectations, supported by appropriate evidence.
  • Possible adverse effects, with meaningful denominators if rates are used.
  • Costs and any supported comparisons, with currency, date and included items clear.
  • What an international patient needs to prepare.
  • Relevant FAQs; the original plan proposes eight to ten when useful.

The original examples include botulinum toxin, fillers, skin boosters such as Rejuran, Ultherapy, InMode, thread lifts, pico lasers and fractional CO2 lasers. Select only services the clinic actually provides, with the correct product or device, clinical evidence and advertising conditions. This list does not establish that every option is suitable for a visitor.

Step 2: monitor relevant AI answers — weekly from week 2

Use a consistent set of questions, such as these ten examples:

1. Best dermatology clinic in Seoul for foreigners
2. Best clinic for [procedure] in Gangnam
3. English speaking dermatologist in Seoul
4. Korean medical tourism dermatology recommendation
5. [Physician's English name] Korea dermatology
6. [Clinic's English name] reviews
7. How safe is botox in Korea
8. Korean skin booster vs Japanese
9. Medical tourism Korea dermatology cost
10. Best K-beauty clinic Gangnam

Record the service, available model, language, location, date, search activation, exact question and repeated responses. Distinguish a name mention from a linked citation, a referral visit and a confirmed inquiry. The examples do not constitute a fresh visibility measurement. Repeatedly missing topics can inform a review, alongside patient questions and actual service priorities.

Keep the actual clinic name, address, physician details, services and English website URL consistent. Consistent, accurate details help patients recognize the clinic across sites. It does not guarantee that an AI service will cite it.

Step 4: build a useful English FAQ library — weeks 4–6

The original plan proposes a pool of 30 questions. Start with genuine needs and put each answer on the relevant page. Examples include:

  • Do I need a visa for medical travel to Korea?
  • How long might I need to stay after this procedure?
  • Does the current tax-refund regime apply to this specific bill?
  • Is interpretation available, and how is it arranged?
  • Who can communicate in English at the clinic?
  • Which payment methods are accepted?
  • How can I request an appointment from abroad?
  • Which documents should I bring?

Two to five sentences can be a useful first draft for a simple answer. Keep necessary conditions, risks and sources even if the answer needs more space. Visa, tax, staffing and payment information must reflect current requirements and the clinic's actual arrangements. FAQPage markup is optional descriptive markup, not a promise of Google rich results or AI citations.

Step 5: review patient stories and advertising — weeks 6–8

A patient story can become medical advertising. Under Medical Service Act Article 56, content that may mislead consumers about treatment effects is prohibited. An anonymous format, a sponsorship disclosure or written consent does not by itself make a testimonial permissible.

Risky approachMore appropriate review direction — not pre-approved wording
“Best clinic in Korea”State a specific, supportable qualification or service. “Internationally recognized” also needs evidence and a permissible context.
A sponsored experience presented as independentDo not disguise sponsorship. Separately assess testimonial restrictions, privacy and any review requirement.
“Guaranteed result”Explain suitability factors and options that may be discussed after assessment.
“100% safe”Describe actual assessment steps and relevant risks without promising that screening removes them.
Unnamed celebrity associationsOmit unsupported associations and do not use identifiable patient information without the necessary rights.

For any proposed patient material, establish the lawful basis and specific consent where required, the intended audience and medium, and the applicable medical-advertising restrictions. Before-and-after photographs require their own review; consent and an “individual results vary” line do not automatically resolve the advertising issues.

4. Registration requirements for attracting international patients

Check the actual registration before running an international patient-acquisition operation. The relevant Korean legislation is the Act on Support for Overseas Expansion of Healthcare System and Attraction of International Patients.

Institution registration requirements

  • Specialists: at least one qualified specialist for each relevant specialty, subject to the statutory exception for departments outside the prescribed specialties.
  • Medical liability cover: the required medical-malpractice liability insurance or membership in the relevant medical indemnity mutual association.
  • Competent city or provincial authority: Article 6 requires registration with that authority. The KHIDI-operated Medical Korea system provides the application route and jurisdiction contacts; registration is not solely a ministerial function.

Attracting patients without registration

Article 28 provides for up to three years' imprisonment or a fine of up to KRW 30 million for attracting foreign patients without the registration required by Article 6. Whether particular conduct falls within the provision needs an assessment of the actual activity.

Checking registration information

The official foreign-patient attraction information system provides registration information and lookup functions. Verify the institution's current record and validity, rather than relying on an old logo or directory entry.

What an English page may state

Use accurate, current statements that the institution can substantiate:

  • Its actual foreign-patient attraction registration status and number, where applicable.
  • The approval number and scope of a particular medical advertisement when required; do not claim that every advertisement has blanket approval.
  • Its actual applicable medical liability cover, described without implying a guarantee of treatment outcomes.

Registration, advertising review, privacy and clinical suitability are separate questions. An English footer cannot settle all four.

5. Make English AI visibility part of a measurable patient-information plan

The 2024 patient figures show substantial international activity. They do not prove that English AI answers are now the first choice for every patient, or that starting early reserves an exclusive slot for the next one or two years.

Begin with a small, relevant review:

This week, choose five English questions about the clinic's actual priority services. Check relevant public search and AI answers, and record whether the clinic is mentioned, which sources are cited and what information the answers lack.

Use that evidence to decide which English explanations or listings to improve. Compare the effort and outcome with other channels using actual inquiries and confirmed appointments. A missing AI mention alone does not establish that AEO will outperform more advertising.
