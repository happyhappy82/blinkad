2. Check What the Profile Tells a Customer

Build a profile someone can use, then keep it accurate when the business changes.

  • Basic information: check the real-world name, precise address, phone, opening hours and relevant website. Show applicable break periods and special hours through the supported controls; optional fields are not universal eligibility requirements.
  • Categories: select the most accurate available primary category and relevant additional ones. Two or three extras are not mandatory. The original Dwaejiya example should not be used to invent a “best samgyeopsal restaurant” category; choose from Google’s list and describe only the real business.
  • Photos and videos: show the exterior, interior, representative menu or service, and useful access details. Five photos were the original starter suggestion, not a ranking threshold. Use appropriate permission for identifiable people and customer material.
  • Updates: share real menu changes, events and notices when useful. Use accurate dates and conditions and allow for Google’s review. A weekly posting pattern does not ensure a particular position or repeat visit.
  • Review replies: read the concern, respond politely and describe only checks or improvements actually made. Avoid publishing personal information or promising to satisfy every future customer. A neutral review link can be offered to genuine customers without rewards, positive-only selection, pressure or requested wording.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does every local business need a Google Business Profile?

It is useful for eligible businesses whose customers use Search or Maps, but eligibility and discovery habits matter. A profile is not mandatory for every business and does not guarantee top placement. Start by finding the correct existing listing and confirming authorized access.

Which photos should I prioritize?

Begin with the sign and entrance, the real interior, and representative products or services. For an upper-floor business, show the building entrance and how to reach the floor where appropriate. The original five-photo suggestion is a manageable starting point, not a Google rule.

How should review replies be managed?

Respond to the actual feedback with relevant, truthful information and protect private details. Ask genuine customers for optional feedback without incentives or selecting only happy customers. Reviews can contribute to local prominence, but Google does not guarantee a rank increase because every review has a reply.
