Record what staff will need for the next conversation

After a consultation, distinguish what the customer actually requested from what staff explained. Briefly record the service or product they were interested in and any question you agreed to revisit. A staff member's guess about a preference should not become a recorded customer request.

After the appointment, record the service actually provided and the products used. Listing something the customer only asked about as a completed service can mislead the next colleague. Continue to check bookings and payments in the system your salon already uses.

Agree on what the next staff member should read first. Reviewing the latest service, most recent conversation and agreed follow-up in the same order can reduce the time spent searching old messages.

When several staff members handle inquiries, check what the customer has already been told. Separate information that does not need repeating from questions still awaiting an answer. The next colleague can then continue the conversation where needed.

Decide why and when to contact the customer again

Before sending a next-visit reminder, review the service provided and what was agreed in the last conversation. A standard interval does not mean that every customer should receive the same message on the same date. Staff should understand why the contact is needed before deciding what to send.

In BeautyNest, staff can review the service record while preparing the next-visit message. Set the timing and content according to the salon's agreed approach and the individual customer's record. Check whether someone has already contacted them, so the same message is not sent twice.

Distinguish visit-related guidance from promotional messages. If you are advertising a new product or offer, check the customer's consent and opt-out status, along with the applicable requirements for sending advertising messages (Korean).
