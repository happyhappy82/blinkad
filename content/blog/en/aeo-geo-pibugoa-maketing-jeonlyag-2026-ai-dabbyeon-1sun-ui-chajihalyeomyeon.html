If someone asks ChatGPT to recommend a dermatology clinic in Gangnam, does your clinic appear? It is a useful question to investigate alongside the ways patients already find and contact you.

An AI mention alone does not decide the success of a clinic's marketing. Neither does it establish that social media, medical platforms or paid search have stopped producing a return. Check the actual journey and results.

This six-step plan helps dermatology clinic owners and marketing teams organize useful information for search and AI services. AEO means answer engine optimization; GEO means generative engine optimization. The terms describe related marketing work, rather than an official promise of first place.

The Korean original was published April 22, 2026. This English edition checks sources and feature information as of October 31, 2026, keeping historical figures separate from current clinic results.

1. Read the market figures with their definitions in view

Market estimates

  • The original article cited a KRW 11.2 trillion forecast for 2031. Its underlying forecast and currency basis were not independently recovered here; do not treat it as measured current Korean clinic revenue.
  • Data Bridge's public Korea aesthetic-market summary projects 17.30% annual growth for its forecast period through 2032. It is a commercial forecast with its own market definition, not a guaranteed growth rate for an individual clinic.
  • The same provider estimates that nonsurgical procedures accounted for 71.5% of its defined market revenue in 2024. This is a commercial estimate, not a share of all Korean healthcare revenue or a measured result for this clinic.

Historical international-patient activity

KHIDI's 2024 report recorded 705,044 dermatology patients, up 194.9%. The 56.6% share uses department-based counts, where someone visiting different departments can be counted in each. It is not 56.6% of all unique international patients. These are 2024 figures, not a current annual total.

The competitive context in Gangnam

The original article cited a news analysis with 95% versus approximately 80% medical representation among a selected set of twenty leading brands. The underlying merchant dataset was not available for this edition. That selected-brand comparison cannot establish that healthcare receives 95% of all Gangnam spending, or that dermatology is the largest beneficiary.

For a clinic, the useful task is to identify the services, languages and questions relevant to its actual patients, rather than infer demand from an incompatible market total.

2. A channel-only plan can leave important questions unanswered

“Increase exposure on a medical platform, sponsor an influencer and raise the paid-search bid.”

Those activities need their own performance and policy review. They may still help, but they do not necessarily provide the explanations a patient needs before deciding to inquire.

ListeningMind's beauty and skincare journey material reports that approximately 89% of the analyzed search activity concerns early exploration or information gathering. That is a category-specific search classification, not the percentage of dermatology patients who choose a clinic through AI.

Plan for the questions patients ask before they are ready to request an appointment. Do not assume that every patient visits ChatGPT before a medical platform, or that absence from one answer breaks every conversion path.

3. Organize the work around three priorities

First, understand questions across the decision process

The original article listed these Korean-language search themes in April 2026. The following are English renderings of those examples, not new autocomplete or volume measurements:

  • Dermatology recommendations in Gangnam, community discussions and recognized dermatology specialists.
  • Where to discuss botulinum-toxin treatment, including clinics in Gangnam.
  • Dermatology marketing strategies and agency recommendations.

Notice that the last group concerns business owners, not prospective patients. Separate the audience and intent before using a topic for a clinic page or visibility check.

Second, use appropriate evidence and actual clinic information

The GEO paper reported visibility improvements up to 40% under its experimental conditions. It did not establish that publishing internal treatment statistics guarantees at least a 40% citation increase for a clinic.

  • Recovery measures: use real records and defined follow-up. The original's 800-patient, 36-hour example is not a verified clinical dataset supplied here.
  • Adverse events: define the event, denominator and follow-up. The original's 1,200-case, 0.4% example is not evidence of a clinic's actual risk.
  • Process and experience: explain actual assessment steps and any properly supported survey findings, with the period and limitations clear.

Original records can be useful, but they require clinical interpretation, privacy safeguards and appropriate publication review. A number does not become a quality guarantee simply because it comes from inside the clinic.

Third, assess medical advertising alongside content usefulness

Review the actual advertiser, content, medium and any exceptions. Recurring questions include:

  • Misleading treatment experiences or sponsored testimonials; the 2024 source's 183/31.7% figure contains a denominator inconsistency.
  • Medical advertising on a covered blog or video platform without the required review.
  • A proposed destination page or version outside the scope of an approved advertisement.
  • Unsupported product or treatment-effect claims, including confusion between a cosmetic and an injected medicine.
  • Unsubstantiated superiority, exclusivity or outcome guarantees.

The wording table below is a review aid. It cannot turn a prohibited claim into a lawful advertisement by replacing a few words.

4. A six-step implementation plan

Step 1: document the current position — week 1

Choose five relevant questions. These are translated planning examples, not a new test result:

1. Dermatology clinics in Gangnam
2. Where to discuss [the clinic's actual priority procedure] in Gangnam
3. [Physician's name] dermatology
4. [Clinic name] reviews
5. Questions about safety when choosing a Gangnam dermatology clinic

Record the available service and model, language, location, date, search activation, exact question and repeated responses. Save mentions and citations separately. A missing topic can inform a review, but should be prioritized alongside real services and patient questions.

Step 2: organize useful information for priority procedures — weeks 2–3

The original plan proposes five services as a manageable starting group. For each, assess what the clinic can substantiate:

  • Actual procedure counts, with scope and period.
  • Defined recovery measures and follow-up.
  • Relevant adverse-event information and limitations.
  • Actual examination and assessment steps.
  • Properly described satisfaction or NPS findings, where available.

There is no requirement to manufacture a dataset when the clinic does not have one. Use suitable published clinical evidence and accurate service information instead.

Step 3: draft the FAQs patients actually need — weeks 3–4

Start with a manageable set of real questions; the original plan proposes thirty, with initial answers of two to five sentences. Neither quantity is an AI eligibility threshold. Retain the conditions and risks needed for an accurate answer.

Risky approachReview direction — not approved wording
“Best results” or “No. 1”Use substantiated facts and explain what a survey or measure actually shows; satisfaction does not prove clinical efficacy.
“Absolutely no adverse effects”Describe actual assessment steps and material risks without implying they are eliminated.
“Guaranteed treatment result”Explain the options and suitability factors assessed during a consultation.
“Popular with famous celebrities”Omit unsupported associations and protect patient information.
A paid experience disguised as independent feedbackAssess the testimonial restriction and lawful use; anonymity or consent alone does not resolve it.

FAQs should serve the reader. Google no longer displays FAQ rich results, and its AI features do not require special FAQPage markup. Any optional markup must match visible, accurate content.

Step 4: consider useful physician explanations on video — weeks 4–6

SE Ranking's health study found YouTube at 4.43% of citations and academic journals at 0.48% in a December 2025 snapshot of 50,807 German-language queries from Berlin. That does not mean a Korean clinic without twelve videos is ineligible for AI answers.

The original plan's twelve roughly five-minute videos are a production option:

  • Five explanations of relevant procedure-safety questions.
  • Five answers to genuine patient questions.
  • Two explanations of the clinic's actual assessment or care processes.

Confirm the physician's actual participation, clinical claims and advertising requirements. Clear descriptions, transcripts and chapters can help people use a video; do not treat them as a universal citation guarantee.

Step 5: prepare English information for international patients — weeks 6–8

The 2024 dermatology figures give historical context for international care. They do not measure current English AI visibility. Check the clinic's registration, actual services and language arrangements before attracting international patients.

  • Priority procedure guides; the original proposes ten, where the services and questions justify them.
  • Useful English FAQs; twenty is a planning suggestion.
  • Accurate information about appointments, interpretation, fees and visa-related preparation.
  • Truthful English physician biographies.

Patients need enough clear information to make an informed inquiry; a page count cannot tell you whether they have it.

Step 6: review changes and correct information — ongoing

A ninety-day review cycle can be a practical internal routine. Ahrefs' 76.4% freshness finding uses a detectable-date subset of its top-cited pages, not all citations in a separate 17-million-citation study.

  • Use a monthly review to identify information that may need attention.
  • Update statistical claims only when actual records or evidence have changed.
  • Add clinical material only with appropriate rights, evidence and review.
  • Address new questions or services when they are relevant and actually supported.

Record substantive changes and truthful dates. Do not refresh a date or create patient cases merely to satisfy a schedule.

5. Seven questions for the clinic's review

Use these to identify work, rather than score a guaranteed AI recommendation. A serious unsupported claim matters even if the other checks pass.

  • Have relevant AI and search answers been observed under documented, repeated conditions?
  • Are useful procedure explanations and any internal figures properly supported, rather than created to meet a five-page quota?
  • Do FAQs answer real questions, without treating ten schema-marked pages as an inclusion requirement?
  • Would clinician video explanations help this audience, without assuming twelve videos are mandatory?
  • Does the English information answer international patients' practical questions, rather than target thirty pages for their own sake?
  • Have the applicable medical-advertising review requirements and exceptions been assessed, with valid approvals where needed?
  • Are changing facts and real revisions recorded, without requiring an arbitrary 30% of pages to carry recent dates?

6. Measure what the content helps patients do

AI mentions, citation rates and matching a question are useful observations. Multiplying them does not produce a validated return-on-investment figure. Review actual inquiries, appointments, appropriate revenue measures and the cost of the work.

More advertising spend may or may not help; the same is true of more content. Begin by identifying a real information gap.

This week, choose five questions about the clinic's actual priority services. Check relevant search and AI answers, record the sources and conditions, and identify which missing or inaccurate information the clinic can responsibly improve.

Compare the results with other acquisition channels. This plan does not establish that AEO is ten times more efficient than advertising, or guarantee a first recommendation.
