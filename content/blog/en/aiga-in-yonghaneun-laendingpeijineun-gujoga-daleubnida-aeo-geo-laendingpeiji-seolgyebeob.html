Two pages can cover a similar topic while only one appears as a source in an AI answer. Structure is one part of making an answer easy to find and understand, alongside relevance, evidence and access to the page. A useful starting point for an AEO or GEO landing page is to write clear sections that answer a real question and make sense when read on their own.

The original article drew on BlinkAd's work with hospital and local-business content and our observations of AI citations. The principles below are practical editorial guidance. They do not describe a method shared by every AI service or guarantee that a page will be cited.

Principle 1: Give the answer before the explanation

A promotional landing page may build toward its answer through a problem statement and a long introduction. For an answer-focused page, put a direct response near the top and at the start of each relevant section. Begin with two to five sentences, then add the evidence, conditions and explanation. Treat that length as a writing aid, not a citation threshold.

Principle 2: Give each page a clear central question

A reader may rely on the first answer they receive, so the page should resolve its main question without requiring another search. Avoid crowding unrelated subjects into one page. Use a page or a clearly defined section for each distinct need. Separate genuinely different services when they need different explanations, rather than creating thin pages for every slight variation of a question.

Principle 3: Put comparisons and conditions in readable text tables

Tables can make prices, time requirements and suitability conditions easier to compare. Use HTML text for important information rather than placing it only inside an image. This is a readability and accessibility recommendation, not a claim that all AI systems are unable to interpret images or that a table guarantees more citations.

Principle 4: Use the customer's question as a heading

A heading such as “How much does it cost?” tells the reader more directly what the section answers than a vague “Cost information” label. Follow it with a short answer that includes the relevant conditions. This gives the reader a clear question-and-answer section without relying on exact wording as a ranking shortcut.

Principle 5: Make it clear who is responsible for the information

Readers need to judge where information comes from. A landing page should make the following visible where relevant:

  • The actual author or reviewer, with an accurate role and relevant experience.
  • A business name, address and contact details consistent with the Google Business Profile.
  • The date the information was prepared or substantively updated.

Clear source information and evidence help readers assess the page's claims. Do not invent credentials or change the date merely to make unchanged information appear fresh.

Principle 6: Check the technical foundations before publication

Clear content comes before adding tags, but technical access still matters. Check crawlability, indexing eligibility, metadata and page experience as part of publication. Structured data should describe the visible content accurately. Google does not require a special AI schema for its AI search features, and eligibility does not guarantee inclusion. Schema and fast loading cannot replace a useful answer.

A design checklist

Before building the page, work through these questions:

  • Can we state the page's central question in one sentence?
  • Does the opening give a useful answer in roughly two to five sentences?
  • Are important comparisons and numbers available as text?
  • Do clear question headings lead into direct answers?
  • Can readers identify the responsible author or business and the information date?
  • Is a relevant next-action button placed where it follows naturally from the answer?

Frequently asked questions

Should we revise the existing landing page or create a new one?

If the page already addresses one clear need, revise it using the six principles. If it combines unrelated topics, reorganize it into clear sections or separate pages where the questions require distinct answers. Do not split a complete answer simply to create more URLs.

How should we check whether it is working?

Begin with ten important questions for your industry and record whether your page appears as a supporting source over repeated checks. Record the platform, language, date and other relevant conditions for each check, and repeat the questions under comparable conditions. Review citation observations, identifiable referral visits, brand-search trends and verified inquiries separately. Clicks alone give an incomplete picture, and changes in those measures do not by themselves prove that a page edit caused the result.

Can a medical business use the same approach?

The principles can help organize medical information, but the content needs an appropriate medical and advertising review. Korea's Medical Service Act, Article 56 (Korean) restricts misleading treatment claims and certain patient-experience advertising. Explain relevant conditions and individual variation accurately; a disclaimer alone does not establish that an advertisement is compliant.
