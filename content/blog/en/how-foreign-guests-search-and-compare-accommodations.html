Marketing accommodation to international guests means connecting the information they need to discover a property, understand its rooms, location and conditions, book, and then find their way there. For a guesthouse or a hanok stay—a stay in a traditional Korean house—atmosphere photographs need practical details alongside them: bathrooms, stairs and check-in.

This article focuses on independent accommodation such as guesthouses and hanok stays. Comparing rooms and rate conditions on Agoda and Trip.com is covered in our hotel marketing guide. Here, we look at how a small property's operator can explain the details they know firsthand to international guests.

What information belongs on the screens guests use to search?

A booking platform can be a starting point for accommodation matching dates, guest numbers and an area. An interested guest may then inspect nearby transport on a map or search the property name for official details. Giving each channel a clear information role helps the operator see what is missing.

Which guesthouse and hanok details need explaining?

Descriptions such as “cozy space” or “convenient location” do not tell guests enough to judge whether a property suits their trip. Describe differences you can verify in the actual space.

For a guesthouse, make clear whether a booking is for a private room or one bed. Photographs and text must agree on whether the bathroom is inside the room or shared. If there is a shared kitchen or lounge, explain its hours and how to use it.

For a hanok stay, explain bedding, bathroom location, thresholds, stairs and access to the courtyard and shared spaces for the actual room. Do not let the name “hanok” suggest every room has the same layout. Clearly distinguish rooms with beds from those with floor bedding.

Separate general atmosphere photographs from room-specific explanations. Guests should know which bed, bathroom and entrance belong to the room they selected. Airbnb's check-in guidance recommends photographs or video to help guests arrive and get inside. Images for choosing a room and images for finding it have different purposes.

Which accommodation is eligible for Google Maps profiles?

If a property is eligible for a Business Profile, maintain the correct name, address, contact details and official link so guests can find it again. Google's hotel-profile guidance mentions specific categories reflecting the actual operation, such as hostel and motel.

Taking accommodation bookings does not give every property the same eligibility. Google's business eligibility guidance excludes rental or for-sale properties such as vacation homes and vacant apartments. Check the actual operation and eligibility before managing a profile. Changing the category name alone does not change eligibility.

For maintaining an eligible profile and connecting visit information, see BlinkAd's Google Maps marketing guide. Where a property restricts its public location details, organize information around the platform's location-disclosure settings and arrival instructions for confirmed guests.

What did the official-source comparison establish?

The Korean article records that BlinkAd compared Google's accommodation-profile and eligibility guidance with Airbnb arrival guidance on September 5, 2026. Two distinctions mattered for operators: what kind of property is being represented, and which information is shown to whom.

Google's category guidance needs to be read alongside eligibility rules. The separate rental-property restrictions can be missed when reading only the hotel-profile page. Our recommendation is to check the actual business model before deciding how to operate a profile, rather than relying on “stay” or “guesthouse” in a property's name.

Airbnb's documentation distinguishes information before booking from instructions after confirmation. Its guidance on information shared when a booking is confirmed explains that confirmed guests can access the host's contact number, full address and arrival details. Rather than prescribing one release time for every detailed instruction, we recommend checking what guests actually see under the platform and booking conditions you use.

What can the operator add firsthand?

The operator can describe observed details more precisely than a copied introduction. Use the actual entrance, room layout and frequently asked questions to improve the information.

Rather than saying only “close to the station,” walk the route and check the correct exit, slopes and stairs. When expressing distance as time, give the starting point and transport method. For a guest pulling luggage, an entrance photograph or information about stairs may matter as much as an image of the atmosphere.

Explain beds, bedding and bathroom location rather than relying on “comfortable sleeping space.” Give shared-space hours too. Concrete operating details help guests judge the fit with their schedule and daily habits.

State both the checked fact and where it applies. If only one room requires stairs, name that room instead of describing the whole property that way. If late check-in requires advance contact, explain the contact and confirmation process.

With those details ready, language-specific guidance becomes more useful. Prepare the Korean operational source, then check that translations convey the same facts. Update both when policies change and show the last check date.

How should a property page support search and AI?

Answer guest questions instead of repeating the property name. Use actual operational questions as headings—whether a bathroom is private, room occupancy or luggage storage before check-in—and answer in the first sentence.

Include the conditions as well as availability. If luggage storage is offered, explain when and where, and link the official contact channel if advance inquiry is needed. Help the guest decide the next step.

Google's AI search guidance keeps the foundations of Search relevant, including useful text, internal links and current business information. Identify the author and check date, and verify that booking and inquiry links work.

A language-specific page should continue through room explanations and contact instructions in that language. Google's multilingual-site guidance covers separate language URLs and language-switching links.

Which accommodation marketing results should you check first?

Record platform views, official-page visits, inquiries, confirmed bookings and actual stays separately. Knowing how far guests progressed helps choose what to improve.

In Google's profile-performance guidance, website clicks count clicks on the website link in the profile. Keep available profile metrics separate from booking records rather than counting them as actual stays.

Also examine completed stays after cancellations, average length of stay, response time and repeated questions. After changing arrival guidance, for example, compare how often guests ask about directions or entry relative to the number of stays. Record changes in occupancy and seasonal conditions alongside the comparison.

Frequently asked questions

What should a small property explain in another language first?

Start with room layout, private or shared bathrooms, check-in time, location, booking and contact. Separate information for choosing the property from arrival instructions after confirmation, and begin with languages staff can actually support.

What if the platform and official website disagree?

Use the rooms and policies actually offered now as the basis for corrections. Align basic facts such as name and room layout. Show rate-specific conditions, including breakfast and cancellation, with the relevant rate plan. Link to the current booking screen for prices and availability.

What is needed to accept direct bookings?

Prepare room availability, payment methods, cancellation and change terms, confirmation instructions and inquiry handling. If starting with inquiries, decide when and where guests will be told whether the reservation is confirmed. Improve information and replies using records from inquiry through completed stay.

Open your public property pages alongside the arrival instructions you send guests. Check whether someone can choose the right room and then find their way to the property.
