“If Naver is already working for us, do we need to manage Google reviews too?”

It is a fair question when time is limited. Our advice is to look at how your customers find and choose the business, then decide where better information and genuine reviews would help. Google Maps reviews and Naver Place reviews can both matter, but neither platform turns a review count directly into sales.

The original discussion combined references to InternetTrend and Statcounter. Different measurement methods, devices and periods cannot simply be joined to establish a single crossover date. Search-engine traffic share also does not measure Google Maps usage, unique customers or revenue. Browser search defaults vary by device and setting; they do not establish a person’s choice of mapping app.

Google is worth checking because your customers may use it. That applies to Korean customers as well as visitors from overseas. The original claim that almost every foreign tourist relies on Google Maps is too broad: Naver offers multilingual maps and review translations, and its June 2026 announcement also described services for international visitors.

The practical point: check whether people using Google can find accurate details and meaningful feedback about your business. A national search-share percentage does not tell you what proportion of your potential customers you are losing.

Which Platform Should Different Businesses Prioritize?

Your industry is a starting point. Your customers’ search and booking habits tell you where to spend your time.

Tourism and Accommodation

A hotel, guesthouse or restaurant near a visitor destination should check its Google presence carefully. Also check Naver and other services your guests actually use. International visitors do use Naver, and a review is only one part of the decision to book.

Restaurants, Cafés and Bars

Both platforms can be useful. In areas such as Itaewon, Hongdae, Myeong-dong and Haeundae, consider the mix of residents and visitors and the languages they need. The original example of a 4.5 Naver score versus 3.8 on Google does not establish that one platform contains sponsored reviews and the other contains only genuine visits. Different samples and rating systems can produce different scores.

There is also a date-sensitive change: Naver says eligible visit records created from April 6, 2026 can receive star ratings, with individual and business-average ratings displayed from July 9, 2026. Supported categories, display settings and a minimum of ten rating contributors affect average-score visibility. Do not treat a missing average as a zero score or compare unmatched periods.

Hair and Beauty Services

If customers use Naver Booking, the Place page is an important part of their journey. If you also serve international customers, make Google information and genuine feedback useful to them. Check which route leads to actual inquiries and confirmed appointments.

Hospitals and Clinics

A clinic serving international patients should consider the services those patients use, including Google. Naver information may also matter to local patients. Review handling requires particular care with privacy and applicable medical-advertising rules; do not publish patient details or promise treatment outcomes in an owner reply.

Everyday Local Services

A laundry, repair shop or academy may find many customers through Naver. Confirm that in your own inquiries rather than assuming a universal platform preference. Keeping an eligible Google profile accurate can still help customers who search there.

A useful check: even if few international customers visit today, search for your business on Google and see whether its details are correct. The original 44% search-share estimate is not proof of the size of your local customer opportunity.

What Can Be Missed When Google Is Left Unattended?

Rather than assuming all customers behave the same way, check four possible gaps.

  • Visitors who use Google: the Korean article referred to more than 16 million international arrivals in 2025. An arrivals total does not mean every visitor uses Google Maps or is a potential customer for your particular business.
  • Relevant Google searches: local map results can help people discover a business. Missing from a displayed map group is worth investigating, though it does not mean the business has no other Search visibility.
  • Customers seeking detailed feedback: keep the information useful on either platform. The article supplied no survey establishing that an entire age group trusts Google reviews more.
  • People researching the brand from overseas: an accurate Google presence can support that research, alongside your website and other relevant sources.

The original article also described a Seongsu brunch café moving from roughly page three on Naver to a top-three Google map result after collecting over 40 reviews and replying regularly, with weekend bookings reportedly rising about 25%. The underlying rankings and booking records were not provided. Those figures cannot establish a review threshold, a causal effect or which platform would be faster for another café.

A Manageable Way to Look After Both Platforms

You can reuse accurate business information while adapting to each platform’s features. A short routine is easier to sustain than trying to do everything at once.

Step 1: Check the Basics

  • Check the correct Google Business Profile, real-world name, address, hours, categories and useful photos.
  • Bring the corresponding Naver SmartPlace details up to date.
  • Fix material conflicts, such as a wrong phone number or closed-day schedule. Minor formatting differences alone do not establish an automatic ranking penalty.

Step 2: Offer an Optional Review Route

  • Place clearly labeled Google and Naver review QR codes where customers can choose to use them.
  • Explain the options in a language the customer understands. Let their preference guide the choice rather than assigning a platform by nationality.
  • Do not offer a drink upgrade, discount or other benefit for a Google review. Calling it “feedback” does not change the rule when the reward is tied to a public review. Use a neutral, unrewarded invitation.

The original article links to five ways to invite customer reviews and a related video (Korean). Follow current platform policy wherever older examples differ.

Step 3: Make Replies Part of the Routine

  • Check new reviews daily or every other day if that fits your volume. Ten minutes may cover a quiet day; a serious concern deserves more time.
  • Thank customers when there is something useful to acknowledge, without turning every reply into a promotion.
  • Address criticism calmly, explain what can be done and move private details to an appropriate private conversation.
  • Use natural language. “Thank you for visiting our Gangnam izakaya” may fit a particular reply, but repeating service and place names is not a published Google SEO formula.

Step 4: Read for Patterns Each Month

  • Compare recurring themes, questions and complaints; compare ratings only where the period and rating system are understood.
  • Use that feedback to identify menu or service improvements. Confirm sales and visits through appropriate business records instead of equating more reviews with more revenue.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I do about an abusive or false Google review?

Report a review that appears to violate Google’s content policy through the profile or review-management tools. A low rating or disagreement alone does not qualify it for removal, and no fixed removal date is assured. Reply calmly where helpful and avoid sharing personal information. Policy review and a customer-service response are different steps.

How many Google reviews do I need?

There is no published universal requirement of 20 or 30 reviews for appearing in the top local results. Nor does a set of 20 recent reviews automatically outrank 50 older ones. Review volume and positive ratings can help, but local relevance, distance, prominence and competition also matter. Continue offering genuine customers an optional chance to share their experience.

What if I can only focus on one platform first?

Begin where your current customers most often discover and contact you, while correcting essential information on both where possible. A business serving nearby residents may prioritize Naver; one receiving substantial Google inquiries may prioritize Google. Verify that pattern before committing more time. Neither nationality nor a claim of lower competition settles the question for every business.

Check the Gap Before Adding More Work

If you have focused on Naver for years, checking Google may reveal missing information you can improve quite quickly. That is a worthwhile first task, even before making a larger marketing decision.

Google Business Profile itself is free for eligible businesses. Preparing basic details may take around ten minutes, but verification and ongoing management can take longer. Start by checking the listing that already exists.
