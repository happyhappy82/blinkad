“We already manage Naver reviews. Do we need to look after Google reviews too?” For a business owner, that means another place to maintain. At BlinkAd, we recommend beginning with where customers discover the business, what they read and how they get in touch.

Google Maps and Naver Place reviews each help customers assess a business on their respective platforms. Search-engine market share cannot be translated directly into your store’s sales share, and more reviews alone do not establish more visits.

Where do customers find your business?

Search for the business name and, separately, the area and category. Look at what appears for customers who already know the name and those still choosing somewhere to visit.

For international customers, try the relevant languages too. Instead of assuming every foreign visitor uses one map, ask actual customers which search, review or recommendation led to their inquiry.

If the opening hours or menu are wrong, correct that information before asking for more reviews. Compare both platforms with the business’s current guidance.

What should you compare between Google Maps and Naver Place?

Google’s policy requires reviews based on genuine experiences and prohibits manipulated or incentivized reviews. Naver’s visitor-review guidance describes reviews associated with reservations, orders and receipts. Naver also operates a policy against fake visits and manipulated reviews.

The existence of a policy does not establish the authenticity of every individual review. Examine the reviews of your business and the underlying customer experience rather than assuming one platform contains only genuine feedback.

Setting priorities by business type

Tourism and accommodation

Check whether room, facility, check-in and directions information matches the actual service on the booking and map pages customers compare. Record which reviews were mentioned in booking inquiries.

Restaurants and cafés

Begin with menus, price information, opening hours and recurring complaints. Where international visitors are customers, check whether they can read the menu and practical visiting information.

Hair and beauty services

Look at whether a customer reading the reviews can select and book the relevant service. Explain the actual service clearly so that expectations and what is provided remain aligned.

Hospitals and clinics

Medical advertising and personal information require separate consideration. The clinic team should check the relevant requirements before using testimonials or running review requests. Our medical advertising review guide explains areas to examine.

Local everyday services

Make the address, service area and usage information clear first, then allocate effort using actual inquiry records. A business category alone does not establish which channel performs better.

What can Google review management help you examine?

Google identifies relevance, distance and prominence as main local-ranking factors and mentions review numbers and positive ratings. It does not provide one minimum review count for every area or a promised ranking lift from adding keywords to replies.

Check the definitions in Business Profile performance. Calls measure clicks on the call button; website clicks measure clicks on the website link. Connected conversations and confirmed bookings require separate records.

For a before-and-after comparison, use matching periods and measures and record other changes, including advertising, hours and menus. Attributing a sales change specifically to reviews requires suitable evidence.

Four steps for managing both platforms

1. Align the basic information

Compare addresses, opening hours, phone numbers and menus with actual operations. If bookings happen elsewhere, check that customers can reach the correct destination.

2. Invite voluntary reviews from customers with genuine experiences

Google explains how to share a review link or QR code. Allow customers to describe their experience freely rather than requiring a particular rating or only positive comments.

Offering discounts, free goods or services in exchange for a public Google review violates its policy. Calling the request “feedback” does not change an arrangement that links the public review to a reward. Keep separate feedback collection that is not tied to review publication distinct in its purpose and explanation.

3. Reply to what the customer actually wrote

Thank customers for specific positive feedback. For a complaint, explain what can be checked and how to follow up. Avoid disclosing personal information or posting an explanation that does not match the facts.

4. Examine recurring feedback alongside actual inquiries

Collect repeated concerns and questions about menus or services, as well as the review count. Update customer guidance accordingly and compare inquiry and booking records to identify information that is still missing.

Frequently asked questions

Can we remove an unfavorable review?

Google provides reporting procedures for policy violations. Check the actual violation rather than expecting removal merely because a review is negative. Naver also provides defined checking and reporting procedures, rather than allowing owners to hide all visitor reviews at will.

How many reviews are needed to see an effect?

There is no single useful number for every business. Consider whether customers have enough relevant information, whether it reflects current operations and whether inquiries are being received.

What if we can manage only one platform first?

Use current customer and inquiry paths and the information gaps to set the priority. Keep essential information, such as the address and opening hours, accurate on the other platform too.
