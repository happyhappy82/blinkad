“The address and phone number are correct. Why can’t I finish verification?”

A missing postcard, an unavailable phone option or a rejected video can make the last part of setting up a Google Business Profile feel confusing. The right next step depends on the status shown for the actual profile; someone else’s verification screen may offer different choices.

This guide explains the available methods and seven common situations to check when Google Business Profile verification is not progressing.

What Does Verification Let You Do?

Verification helps establish that you are authorized to represent the business. It gives you access to management functions, but it is useful to separate management access from whether a public listing already exists.

Think of verification as confirming your authority to manage the business. It does not create a guaranteed top position or mean that every unverified listing is absent from Google.

Which Verification Methods Might Be Offered?

Google determines the available options using information about the business and other factors. Use the methods shown on your own profile. In some cases, more than one method may be required.

1. Mail

Where offered, a postcard contains a verification code for the business address. Google currently says most mailed codes arrive within 14 days. The Korean article’s 14-to-21-day estimate is not a universal delivery period or maximum. Check the actual process shown if the code does not arrive.

2. Phone or Text

If offered, you must be able to receive the call or text at the displayed business number. This option is not available to everyone, and an automated telephone menu can prevent receipt of a code.

3. Email

If Google offers email verification, use the address displayed in that process. Having a website or a business-domain email does not guarantee that the option will appear.

4. Video Recording

This method asks you to record evidence of the business and your authority to manage it through the profile on a mobile device. The original article described its expansion from 2023 and called it the most commonly requested method, but supplied no distribution data. Follow the recording option where it is actually offered.

5. Live Video Call

Some profiles may be offered a live call to show the location and evidence of management. Availability and business hours matter. It is different from submitting a video recording.

A missing phone or email option does not, by itself, indicate a fault. Completing accurate information is useful, but does not guarantee that Google will unlock your preferred method.

Seven Common Problems, with a Next Step for Each

Problem 1: The Postcard Has Not Arrived

Situation: you requested a mailed code and have been waiting longer than expected.

What to check:

  • Confirm that the address represents the real, eligible business and can receive mail. Receiving mail at a virtual office alone does not establish eligibility; coworking locations have additional conditions.
  • Check the relevant mailbox or reception point at the building.
  • If the code is overdue, follow the available verification or support instructions. The original 30-day waiting suggestion is not a universal requirement before you can ask for help.
  • Do not repeatedly request new codes while one is on the way. A new request invalidates the previous code; use the valid current code and check its status.

Problem 2: Phone or Email Is Missing

Situation: another owner could verify by phone, but your profile offers a different route.

What to check:

  • Read the methods available for your business rather than comparing screens from different profiles.
  • Check that the existing details accurately describe the business. Extra fields alone do not guarantee another verification option.
  • Use an offered method, or follow Google’s support route if you cannot complete it. Do not assume you can switch to video or mail when neither is offered.

Problem 3: A Video Recording Was Not Accepted

Situation: the profile reports a problem with the submitted recording.

What to check:

  • For a storefront, show permanent signage that clearly identifies the actual business.
  • Show nearby location evidence, such as the street or building number.
  • Show appropriate business equipment, products or the working space that demonstrates the business exists.
  • Demonstrate management access, such as unlocking a staff-only area. Use non-sensitive documents only where appropriate; do not record tax, bank or identification numbers, private information or other people’s faces.
  • Follow the specific rejection feedback and make a clear, continuous recording through the required mobile process. Google requires at least 30 seconds, without edits or breaks.
Plan the route before recording. Google requires live capture through the verification process; do not edit separate clips together or record a video offline to upload later.

Problem 4: Another Account Owns the Profile

Situation: an existing verified profile is managed by someone else.

What to check:

  • Find the correct existing profile and use the ownership-request route if you are authorized to represent the business. Service-area ownership cases may require the support route described by Google.
  • Google’s current guidance gives the existing owner three days to respond, rather than the seven days in the Korean article. If no one responds, a claim or verification option may appear, but is not guaranteed.
  • Check whether a previous agency or employee already has access and arrange proper role-based access. Do not create a duplicate profile to avoid the ownership issue.

Problem 5: The Code Is Rejected or Has Expired

Situation: the code is not accepted.

What to check:

  • Google says mailed verification codes expire after 30 days. Follow the displayed instructions if a replacement is needed.
  • Check whether a later code request or changes to the business name, address or category invalidated the earlier code.
  • Enter the code exactly as shown and follow any error or retry instructions. The mailed code is numeric, so uppercase versus lowercase is not the relevant issue.

Problem 6: The Profile and Business Evidence Conflict

Situation: the real location or submitted supporting information does not match the profile.

What to check:

  • Use the real-world business name and accurate location, supported by genuine evidence. A registration document is not a mandatory upload in every verification process.
  • If the business has moved, update the relevant business records as required and follow Google’s process for the actual current location. A move can require verification again.
  • Use an accurate, clear address, including the right unit or floor where appropriate. An old parcel-address format and a road-name format are not automatically different premises; resolve material discrepancies rather than treating punctuation as the cause.

Problem 7: You Visit Customers Rather Than Receiving Them at a Shop

Situation: you run an eligible service-area business, such as plumbing, moving or mobile cleaning.

What to check:

  • Provide the genuine business information Google requests for verification, while using the appropriate service-area setup. The business still needs to meet in-person service eligibility.
  • If customers are not received at your home or operating address, hide it from the public profile as appropriate from the outset. You do not need to publish a private address first as a workaround.

Useful Preparation While Verification Is Pending

You can prepare materials even if the profile does not yet allow every edit. If you are waiting for a mailed code, avoid changes that invalidate it.

  • Description: draft a clear introduction within 750 characters, without promotional keyword stuffing or links.
  • Categories: identify the accurate primary category and only relevant additional ones. Two or three secondary categories are not mandatory.
  • Hours: prepare regular and special opening hours.
  • Photos: gather clear views of the location and actual products or work. Three per type is a planning suggestion, not a verification threshold.
  • Services: prepare accurate information for the features available to the business.
  • Website and booking: check suitable current destinations for the supported fields.
Get the information ready while the current verification step runs its course. A “100% complete” profile does not guarantee faster publication, and some changes should wait until the current verification step is finished.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Google charge for verification?

Google Business Profile verification itself is free. A third party may charge transparently for its own assistance, but cannot sell Google verification, bypass the process or guarantee approval. The owner should retain appropriate control of the business account.

I am verified, but I still cannot find the business on Maps. What next?

Check the verification status, public listing, exact business location and any review or restriction notices. Google says verification review can take up to five business days and occasionally longer. The original three-day-to-two-week display estimate is not a universal service promise. Follow the available support route if the expected process has finished and the issue remains.

Is Google My Business verification a different service?

No. Google My Business is the former name of Google Business Profile. The name change was announced in November 2021, with the separate app retired during the later transition. Screens and methods have evolved, so follow current instructions rather than assuming every old verification guide still matches your profile.

If You Still Need Help

Describe the actual status and the step that failed when using Google’s support route.

  • Open the Google Business Profile help flow while signed into the appropriate account.
  • Choose the issue and contact option actually offered. Chat or email availability can vary.
  • Prepare a screenshot of the problem and provide only the business evidence requested through the proper private channel. Do not post identification numbers or verification codes in a public community thread.

Verification can take patience, but checking the real status is more useful than repeatedly changing information or opening new listings. You may also be asked to verify again after significant changes; it is not necessarily a once-only event.

Start with the problem shown on your profile and follow the next instruction Google provides. No guide can guarantee approval for every business.
