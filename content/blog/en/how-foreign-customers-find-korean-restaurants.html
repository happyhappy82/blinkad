Before advertising a restaurant to international customers, check how people discover it, compare it and decide to visit. The aim is to help someone finding the restaurant through Google Search and Maps understand its menu, prices, photographs, reviews and hours, then choose directions or a reservation.

Restaurant marketing often brings influencers and sponsored blog visits to mind first. For international customers, plan for what happens next: someone interested in a post may search for the restaurant again. Without enough official information, their decision can stop before a visit.

How do international customers look for restaurants?

People do not all use the same apps or queries. Nationality, language, travel-planning stage and current location can affect the route. A restaurant can organize its information around four stages:

StageWhat customers checkWhat to prepare
DiscoveryArea and cuisineAccurate category, address and hours
ComparisonPhotographs, menu, prices and reviewsCurrent food, exterior and interior images and menu information
DecisionReservations, waiting, payment and how to use the restaurantInstructions and booking links matching actual operations
VisitLocation, entrance and directionsAn accurate pin and recognizable exterior photographs

A tourist who does not know the name may search by cuisine and location, such as Korean BBQ near me or restaurants near Gyeongbokgung. Translating the business name alone is less useful than making the category, location, menu and practical information agree.

Google's local search guidance identifies relevance, distance and prominence as key factors. It also recommends accurate, complete information, current hours, review replies and photographs.

What should a restaurant review first?

Begin with the Business Profile basics. Check the name, address, phone number, primary category and hours against the actual restaurant. For registration and management steps, see the Google Business Profile management guide (Korean). Keep breaks and closing days current so people do not arrive when the restaurant is closed.

Next, review the menu. Google's restaurant-profile guidance describes menu items, descriptions, prices, menu photographs and a menu URL. Features can vary by region and account, but customers need a clear way to read the menu after discovery. For more detail, see creating an English menu for what international customers see on Maps.

Go beyond a literal translation of dish names. Include the information you can actually verify:

  • Main ingredients and cooking method
  • Portion size or recommended number of diners
  • Current selling price
  • Features to know before ordering, such as spice level
  • The official way to check allergies or vegetarian options

Only describe food as suitable for allergies, halal requirements or vegetarian diets after checking kitchen practices and ingredients. The apparent absence of a particular ingredient is not enough to establish suitability.

Then review photographs. Close-ups of signature dishes alone may not answer a first-time visitor's questions about the entrance, seating, ordering or portion sizes. Give exterior, entrance, interior, signature-dish and menu photographs distinct roles.

The gaps differ by restaurant. Some have limited visibility; others appear in search but have an outdated menu or incorrect booking link. Identify the missing stage between discovery and arrival before assuming that international advertising will increase customers.

What should a language-specific page explain?

Give each page its own URL and provide menu, price, location and booking details in that language. For several languages, consider hreflang and explicit language-switching links so Google can understand the relationships.

Accurate decision-making information matters more than publishing large quantities of automatic translation. If the official site and Business Profile disagree on the name, address, phone, menu or booking details, customers, search engines and AI have difficulty identifying the current information.

The same practical principle applies to AEO and GEO: answer questions such as whether a reservation is needed, what it costs, where it is and what is served, briefly and clearly on the official page.

Which numbers help assess restaurant marketing?

Views alone do not establish visits. Google Business Profile performance guidance explains that verified profiles can show applicable actions such as website clicks, calls and direction requests. A restaurant can record the following monthly:

  • Search terms used to find it
  • Menu views or clicks
  • Direction requests
  • Website and call-button clicks
  • Bookings through supported booking providers

These figures are not all equivalent to visits or revenue. Where possible, compare them with booking systems, phone inquiries and POS records that allow the source to be identified. Record seasonal changes, holidays, events and operating days when comparing campaigns.

Where should international-customer marketing begin?

You do not need several countries and channels at once. Start with:

  • Business Profile ownership and basic information
  • Matching online details to the actual menu, prices and hours
  • Organizing exterior, entrance, seating and signature-dish photographs
  • Choosing one priority language for menu and practical information
  • Selecting important actions—directions, calls, website visits or reservations—to review monthly

Once those details are ready, use influencers, search ads and social content to help interested customers take the next step toward a visit. The best channel depends on the area, cuisine, average spend, booking method and target country; it cannot be established without assessing the business.

Frequently asked questions

Is managing Google Maps enough?

Maps is an important discovery route, but may not be sufficient. Customers may need an official page for menus, prices and reservations, while search, social and booking preferences can differ by target country. Align the profile and official information first, then add channels as needed.

Will an English menu bring more international customers?

An English menu helps people order after arrival. Discovery has a different role. The restaurant also needs to appear in relevant searches, let people check the menu from its profile and provide directions or booking. The menu is one part of that process.

How can we assess the results?

Record a starting baseline and compare monthly directions, menu and booking actions under consistent conditions. Keep notes on profile changes, competition, seasonality and advertising to help interpret the difference.
