Creating a listing is only the beginning. Accurate hours, useful photos and clear service information can help a customer decide whether to visit. The original claim that an unmanaged profile delivers less than half the benefit was not backed by a measurement.

How to Add or Claim a Google Business Profile: Seven Steps

Step 1: Open Google Business Profile

Go to business.google.com and sign in. You can also find and manage the relevant business through Google Search or Maps. Labels may vary by device and account.

Step 2: Find the Business and Check Its Name

Look for an existing listing before creating a new one. Use the real-world name customers recognize from signage and branding, rather than automatically copying a legal registration name that differs from the public business name. If an existing verified profile belongs to someone else, use the ownership-request process instead of creating a duplicate.

Step 3: Choose the Best-Matching Category

Select the available category that most closely describes the main business, whether it is a restaurant, salon or another service. Use additional categories only when they accurately apply. A category is not a place to insert every keyword you want to rank for.

Step 4: Enter the Address or Service Area

If customers visit your premises, enter the correct address and check the map pin. An eligible business that travels to customers can use a service-area setup and hide an address where customers are not received. An online-only business does not become eligible simply by adding a service area.

Step 5: Add Contact and Website Details

Use a business phone number you control where appropriate, and the website that represents the actual business location. If you do not have a website, you can begin without one. Do not assume an Instagram or Naver page is always an acceptable replacement in every website field; add social or booking links through the appropriate supported features.

Step 6: Complete the Verification Offered to You

Google determines the available methods according to the business and other information; you cannot require it to offer a preferred method. Video recording is an important current option that was missing from the original comparison. Where offered, record the real location, business and evidence that you manage it, following Google’s instructions. Google says review of the recording can take up to five business days.

Five Useful Tasks After Registration

Once you have access, work through the details a customer is likely to check first.

1. Add representative photos and a logo

  • Choose a cover image that represents the business accurately; Google may display another photo in some contexts.
  • Prepare a clear logo in a suitable square format.
  • Three exterior and three interior photos can be a practical collection to prepare, rather than a mandatory ranking count.

2. Explain the business clearly

  • Use the description to explain the services or products, within the 750-character limit.
  • Mention the actual location naturally, such as a men’s salon near Gangnam Station. Avoid promotional keyword lists.

3. Keep hours accurate

  • Set regular hours and use special hours for holidays or temporary changes where appropriate.
  • Represent breaks and split opening periods accurately so customers do not arrive when you are closed.

4. Add services or menu information

  • Where available, add current dishes, services and accurate prices.
  • The original article claimed that displaying prices raises click-through by an average of 25%, but supplied no study. Give customers clear prices because they are useful, without assuming a fixed uplift.

5. Share a first update

  • A useful post can introduce a new product or explain a relevant change; recent activity is not a published standalone ranking rule.
  • A short opening message can be a start, provided it contains something customers need to know.
The Korean article described a salon receiving phone inquiries within two weeks of completing these tasks, compared with another shop receiving little response after a month. No call records or controlled comparison were provided. Treat it as the original account, rather than a guaranteed two-week result or proof that setup alone caused the difference.

A Weekly and Monthly Management Routine

Ongoing management helps keep information useful as the business changes. It is worth doing, but a weekly schedule cannot by itself determine rankings or guarantee continued growth.

A Weekly Check: Start with Around Ten Minutes

  • Share worthwhile news: one post a week may suit your business. Google does not publish a rule granting better placement simply because a post is less than seven days old.
  • Read and reply to reviews: aim to respond promptly when a reply is useful. A 24-hour target is a service choice, not an official ranking requirement. Avoid incentives, pressure and requests for particular review wording.
  • Check customer questions: use the features available to your region and profile. Keep common answers in your website or other suitable customer information as well.

A Monthly Review: Allow Roughly Thirty Minutes to Begin

  • Check performance: compare search terms, website clicks, call-button clicks and direction requests. Clicks and requests do not confirm completed calls or visits.
  • Refresh photos where needed: show current products and the place customers will see. Do not assume a new upload carries a guaranteed freshness bonus.
  • Check information accuracy: review hours, contact details and the address, especially before holidays.
  • Look at relevant local competitors: learn what their information helps a customer understand, without copying material or assuming the visible differences explain their ranking.
The original article described a neighborhood café posting weekly and replying to reviews for three months, then appearing among the top three for a station-area café search without extra advertising spend. No underlying ranking history, location settings or visit data were supplied. It is not evidence that every café following the same schedule will receive the same result.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Google My Business registration free?

Yes. Google Business Profile itself is free for eligible businesses. Google Ads spending is separate, and an agency may charge for its own work. Registering the profile does not purchase a better organic position.

Can I register without uploading a business registration certificate?

A certificate is not a universal sign-up upload, but Google may request evidence appropriate to your verification process. The business must still meet eligibility rules. A freelancer or home-based service may qualify if it provides in-person services; a service-area setting is not a route around the exclusion of online-only businesses. Prepare suitable evidence and follow the instructions shown, keeping sensitive information private.

Will the business appear on Maps immediately?

A listing may already exist before you claim it. Verification and changes can take time, and neither a 24-to-72-hour display promise nor two to four weeks of management guarantees growth. Follow the actual review status, check the public details and assess relevant customer actions. Ranking also depends on the search, location, prominence and competition.

If you already manage Naver SmartPlace, an accurate Google profile can help customers who look for you there too. Naver also supports international users, so choose priorities from actual customer behavior rather than assuming every visitor or age group uses only one service.

  • Find, add or claim the correct profile. Preparing details may be quick; verification can take longer.
  • Complete the information customers need. Accurate photos, hours and services are useful before a visit.
  • Keep it current. Review changes and customer feedback, then use appropriate records to assess inquiries and visits.

Open business.google.com and check the profile’s current status. If it already exists, start by correcting any important gaps.
