Read the Original Local Figures in Context

Google Business Profile is free for eligible businesses that meet customers in person. Authorized owners can maintain information associated with Search and Maps. Local results depend mainly on relevance, distance and prominence; a profile does not guarantee first position or a sale.

  • Local-business share: the source listed 4,211 businesses, with 89.3% classified as local and 10.7% as franchises. It provided no observation date, raw register or classification method for independent rechecking. The figures do not establish customer preference or brand awareness.
  • Larger categories: Korean food had 515 businesses, hair and beauty 371, and real-estate services 309. These are source category counts rather than the number competing for a particular Google query.

Prepare the Information a Customer Needs

1. Start with the Customer’s Question

  • Service and area: “Korean restaurant in Nohyeong-dong” or “Nohyeong-dong salon” may suggest what needs explaining. These are English planning examples, not newly measured Korean queries.
  • A meaningful detail: a stew, men’s perm or apartment-sales service should appear only if actually offered. A more detailed phrase does not automatically have low competition or a higher conversion rate.
  • A particular situation: someone looking for lunch or an available salon may need current hours and booking arrangements. Do not infer an immediate purchase from the wording alone.

2. Describe the Actual Business

  • Core facts: use the recognized real-world name, actual eligible address or service area, working phone, current hours and relevant website. The source’s I Setak Nohyeong ground-floor laundry example is not a verified current client or address.
  • Description: explain actual services within Google’s 750-character limit, without repeated keywords, links or sales promotions. The source’s ten-year history, fresh Jeju ingredients and solo-dining example needs evidence before use.
  • Photos and video: show the real entrance, space, products or service, with suitable permissions. Ten photos can be a starting plan rather than a ranking requirement. Genuine salon work examples need appropriate consent.
  • Categories: select an accurate available primary category and only relevant additional ones, rather than adding unrelated categories to cover more searches.
  • Reviews: invite genuine customers neutrally and leave participation optional, without incentives, positive-only selection, pressure or requested wording. Reply factually, protect private information and explain only supported improvements.
  • Posts: use supported features for real news, menus, events or offers with current terms. Check publication status and correct changed hours in their own field as well. Frequency alone does not establish a ranking bonus.

3. Decide What Needs Attention First

  • Access: check eligibility, look for the existing profile and confirm the appropriate owner’s access. Follow Google’s available verification methods; they are automatically determined and may include video or more than one method.
  • Core information: confirm the actual name, location, phone, website and hours.
  • Description: explain the real service in ordinary language rather than repeating search phrases.
  • Categories: choose the most accurate primary category and the fewest relevant additional ones. One or two extras are a source suggestion, not a required count.
  • Photos: plan representative permitted views. Ten is an editorial target rather than a Google ranking threshold.
  • Replies: assign a workable process for reading feedback and replying where useful. No reply quota guarantees trust or ranking.
  • News: a weekly post is a planning suggestion, not a requirement to invent promotions.
  • Common questions: answer recurring issues through the website or currently supported profile features. The former Q&A API ended on November 3, 2025; that does not establish identical question features on every profile.
  • Service areas: use them when the business actually visits or delivers to customers under Google’s service-area or hybrid-business rules. Do not add an area merely to cover another search. A business that does not receive customers at its address should follow the applicable address-hiding guidance.

Keep the Details Current as the Business Changes

  • Align the facts: resolve contradictory names, addresses, phones or hours across relevant channels. Legitimate formatting can differ; consistency alone does not establish a fixed ranking benefit.
  • Correct changes promptly: update essential information when it changes and replace misleading content.
  • Learn from public examples: nearby profiles can reveal unanswered questions or useful presentation ideas. Do not copy content or treat a single search result as a stable ranking measurement.
  • Read smaller counts carefully: communications-equipment repair, other professional/scientific services and beverage retail each had three businesses in the source. That does not establish easy entry, unmet demand or a disproportionate return from profile management.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does it differ from Naver SmartPlace?

They serve different search and map platforms with their own management arrangements. Choose priorities from how actual customers find and contact the business. Maintaining one does not establish improved performance on the other.

Why spend time on reviews?

Genuine experiences can answer prospective customers’ questions and reveal concerns worth checking. Helpful replies explain supported facts and keep private details private. Reviews can contribute to prominence, but no particular score or reply routine guarantees a rank, visit or sale.

How can an owner develop search ideas?

Start with actual customer questions and relevant service language. Autocomplete can suggest ideas rather than measure enquiries.
