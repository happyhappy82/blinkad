A patient may find the service information they need, only to have a contact button cover a form field or finish a booking request without knowing whether it was received. When commissioning a website, include what happens after submission in the review. Agree which checks the web agency and your hospital's inquiry team will carry out together so you can find gaps before launch.

Agree what an inquiry and a confirmed booking mean

Decide which channels your hospital will actually operate: phone, an online inquiry form or an external booking service. More buttons will not help if nobody is responsible for responding. First agree each channel's operating hours, who checks incoming requests and the response arrangements, then reflect those decisions in the page wording.

“Your inquiry has been received” and “Your appointment is confirmed” mean different things. If staff must review a request before agreeing a time, explain that process on both the request and completion screens. Entering a preferred time should not trigger a booking-confirmed message by itself. Likewise, record a click on a call button separately from an actual call or a confirmed booking.

Check the full process on phones and in each supported language

On mobile, check that fixed buttons at the bottom do not cover text, fields or consent options. People should still be able to find the next field and submit button when the keyboard is open. Include checks for clear error instructions and for duplicate requests when someone goes back and returns to the form.

If you offer pages in other languages, make sure the service information has not been translated while the inquiry screens remain in Korean. Error messages, acknowledgment messages, response languages and the actual scope of interpreting support must match hospital operations. Avoid wording that suggests immediate help in a language your team does not support. Agree with the agency which screens to test by phone type and language before the review.

Design mock-ups alone cannot show all of this. Before speaking with BlinkAd about your hospital website, outline the contact channels you need and how staff handle requests internally. This gives you something concrete to discuss when deciding what to build and how far testing should go.

Agree the test procedure with your hospital team

Opening a form from a contact button is only the start. Check input-error messages, the submission result, receipt by the responsible staff member and the follow-up instructions shown to the patient. If you use an external booking service, also distinguish the website agency's scope from the external provider's responsibilities.

Before testing, agree the time, accounts and contact details to use, notification recipients and how to delete test data afterward. Use a separate test environment where possible. If the live site must be used, use staff-controlled test contact details and only the minimum dummy content needed. Do not copy real patients' personal or health information, or enter details that send messages or notifications to third parties. Do not take a real appointment slot or run a test that incurs a charge without prior approval.

Review form fields and analytics separately

Identify what the hospital genuinely needs for an initial inquiry. Before routinely asking for detailed medical histories or photographs, consider whether these need a separate collection process. Have required and optional fields, collection purposes, retention and deletion, access permissions and consent reviewed by your hospital's privacy lead or legal adviser. Data sent to external tools, including any transfers abroad, also needs privacy and legal review appropriate to the locations and conditions in which you operate. An agency's standard form does not replace that review.

For analytics, consider distinct actions such as a contact-button click and a successful submission. Design the tracking so it does not send names, contact details, free-text responses, health information or booking details. Check whether entered values are being added to URLs or event names and passed to the analytics tool. Google Analytics' data protection guidance prohibits sending personally identifiable information. Removing identifiers alone does not establish that health information or booking details are appropriate to send to analytics.

Include operating instructions in the handover

Ask for the final deliverables to include the person responsible and operating arrangements for each contact channel, mobile and language test results, messages for requests received and appointments confirmed, and the privacy-review findings and unresolved items. Also keep a list of analytics events and the information that must not be collected.

After launch, decide who will check submission errors and respond to missed inquiries. Prepare any notices that need changing when staff responsibilities change or an external booking service goes down. The review should continue far enough to confirm that staff can receive each request and act on it.
