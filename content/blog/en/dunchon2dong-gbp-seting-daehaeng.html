For a business in Dunchon 2-dong, setting up Google Business Profile begins with a practical question: can a new customer understand what you offer and find the right place?

We will work through the basic information, service descriptions, photos and reviews that help answer it. The aim is a profile customers can use, with a maintenance routine the business can realistically keep.

What Customers Need from the Profile

Google Business Profile lets eligible businesses manage information on Search and Maps without a Google profile subscription charge. It can support local discovery, but registering or hiring an agency does not reserve a top position or ensure a visit.

The source article, published February 22, described 906 businesses in Dunchon 2-dong, with 94.6% classified as local businesses. Its underlying register, collection date and classification method were not available for this edition. Treat these as the original article’s figures, not a verified current census or evidence of customer search behavior.

The useful decision is which information a prospective customer needs: a precise location, actual opening hours, a menu or service explanation, photos and an appropriate way to contact you.

Set Up the Profile Around the Actual Business

1. Understand What the Searcher Needs

Start with real services and customer questions. The following phrases express the meaning of the original examples; they are not newly measured search terms.

  • Location-and-service examples: “real estate in Dunchon 2-dong,” “Korean food in Dunchon 2-dong,” or “hair salon in Dunchon 2-dong.” The source also suggested a florist on Myeongil-ro, a café on Jinhwangdo-ro and a clinic near Dunchon Prugio. Use a location only if it accurately describes the business.
  • The need behind the phrase: the original listed real-estate services at 90 businesses (9.9%), Korean food at 89 (9.8%) and hair and beauty at 77 (8.5%). A property customer may need rental options and contact details; a diner may need lunch, group-dining or takeaway information; a salon customer may need a particular cut or color service. The counts do not establish current demand or permission to advertise services you do not offer.

Explain the relevant service naturally. Repeating the neighborhood name throughout the profile does not make the information more accurate.

2. Complete the Information That Matters

  • Name, address, phone and hours: use the real-world business name and correct contact details. Keep holiday changes current, use the correct address or service-area arrangement, and add a relevant website link where available.
  • Categories: choose the most accurate primary category from Google’s available list. Add others only where they genuinely describe the business. The original two-to-three suggestion is not a mandatory minimum, and individual dishes or “best restaurant” phrases are not custom category options.
  • Description: explain what you actually offer within Google’s 750-character limit. The original 250-character target and two or three keyword mentions are editorial suggestions, not Google requirements. For example, a restaurant on Myeongil-ro might describe its actual Korean lunch dishes and takeaway options. Do not claim seasonal ingredients, specialties or daily availability unless they are true; keep links and special-offer sales copy out of the description.
  • Photos and videos: show the real entrance, sign, interior and representative products or service setting, with appropriate permission. Five useful photos can be a starting collection rather than a ranking condition. The original ground-floor share of 42.4% does not establish passing trade on Myeongil-ro or Jinhwangdo-ro; choose exterior photos because they help people recognize the actual shop.
  • Reviews: invite genuine customers to give optional, honest feedback without gifts, discounts, positive-only selection, pressure or requested wording. Respond to the actual concern, explain only actions you have taken and protect personal information.
  • Posts: share accurate news, offers, events or menu changes where supported. Check dates, conditions and publication status. A regular posting pattern is a work choice, not a promised ranking boost.

3. Keep the Profile Useful as the Business Changes

Once the basics are reliable, decide who will notice changes and keep the profile current.

  • A practical profile checklist: review relevant details rather than chasing a universal “100% complete” score; check the categories, useful photos, recent unanswered feedback and any worthwhile updates. The source’s five-photo, three-month review and weekly-post targets are planning suggestions. Prepare answers to real questions in the channels available, including the website where useful. Do not assume every profile has an editable Q&A section or an agency can use the former bulk Q&A API, which Google ended on November 3, 2025.

Use review replies and updates to inform people, rather than claiming that perfect completion guarantees competitive advantage.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can Profile Management Improve Customer Discovery?

It can help customers read accurate information and take the next step. The source’s “70% more inquiries” claim was not matched to a primary study. Its 35% figure should not be described as a general CTR improvement: Google’s July 2016 article, citing December 2014 Google/Oxera research, compared more website clicks for listings with photos. That historical comparison is not a current forecast for a Dunchon business.

Why Pay Attention to Reviews?

They give customers information about genuine experiences. A useful reply shows that you have read the concern and can explain relevant facts or action, without exposing private details. Reviews can contribute to local prominence, but Google does not publish a guaranteed ranking bonus for replying to every review or reaching a fixed count.

What if I Cannot Manage It Regularly Myself?

Choose a routine you can keep: the original suggested a short daily check and a weekly photo or post review, rather than a Google-mandated schedule. Delegate agreed work where helpful, keep ownership with the business and define approvals and reporting. An agency is an option when its work fits the need and budget.

Make the First Visit Easier to Plan

Confirm the right listing and authorized access, then check the business details, entrance and actual services. Keep the information useful as the business changes, and assess real customer actions without assuming every profile visit becomes a sale.
