After registering a Google Business Profile, it is easy to leave it untouched while other work takes priority. Keeping accurate information matters, but Google does not publish a rule that failing to post automatically makes a business “unmanaged” or lowers its ranking.

One useful feature to consider is Business Profile posts.

Where available, posts let you share news about products, events or business changes without buying Google Ads. Public visibility depends on the profile, content review and Google’s display; it is not guaranteed advertising placement.

What Is a Google Business Profile Post?

A post is a way to share relevant information through the business profile on Search and Maps.

Depending on the post type and interface, a customer may see:

  • A post or update associated with the business information.
  • A photo or video, description, applicable title or dates, and an available action button.
  • A route to more detail or an appropriate website destination.
Google does not charge a media fee to publish a supported profile post. Your preparation time and any outside help are separate costs. A post is different from a paid Google Ads campaign and does not buy a search position.

Post Types and Product Information

Google’s current guide lists Updates, Offers and Events. The original article also discussed products, which should not be assumed to be a fourth post type on every profile.

How to Prepare and Publish a Post: Five Steps

Step 1: Open the Correct Business Profile

  • On a computer, begin at business.google.com or find the business in Search while signed into the account with access.
  • On a phone, use the available Search or Maps business-management controls.

Step 2: Find the Post Option

  • Open the available Posts or Add post control for the correct business.
  • Menu labels can vary; do not rely on an old left-hand dashboard matching every account.

Step 3: Choose the Appropriate Type

An Update is a useful starting point for straightforward news. Choose an Offer or Event when the real information and required dates fit that purpose. Features can vary by business and region.

Step 4: Prepare the Content

  • Photo: use a clear image of the actual subject. The original 1200 × 900-pixel suggestion is a practical preparation size, not an independently established universal post-upload minimum.
  • Title where available: make it describe the actual update or promotion. Offers and Events have specific title and date fields; not every type has the same controls.
  • Description: keep the first lines useful. The original 150–300-character suggestion is an editing target, not a required length.
  • Action: choose an available button and a destination where the customer can complete the promised task, such as reading details or making a booking.

Step 5: Submit and Check the Status

Use the available publication control, then check the result. Google reviews posts: a live post is public, while pending or rejected content is not. Do not assume immediate display without review. If scheduling options appear, they are separate choices; this guide does not set up or publish any business posts for you.

Five Ways to Make the Post Clearer

Tip 1: Choose a Photo That Explains the Subject

A photo can help a customer recognize what the post is about before reading it.

For a restaurant, show the actual dish people can order. The useful question is whether the image represents it clearly, rather than whether photography accounts for a fixed percentage of results.

  • A smartphone can work well with appropriate light and a clear background.
  • For food, try a view that shows the dish and portion accurately.
  • Avoid filling an image with promotional text when it makes the subject harder to see. No universal click-through penalty is established here.

Tip 2: Use Specific Details When They Are True

Use a number when it tells customers something specific and true about the offer.

  • Vague example: “New dishes have arrived.”
  • More specific example: “Three new dishes, with 15% off in the first week,” only if the dishes, period and conditions are real.

Tip 3: Explain the Next Action

Tell customers how an actual offer works, with relevant conditions and a clear destination.

  • “Show this offer when you visit” can explain a genuine redemption process.
  • State the actual start and end dates, rather than repeating “today only” after the offer has expired.
  • If a phone booking includes a benefit, make the terms accurate. Do not connect any benefit to writing, changing or removing a review.

Tip 4: Choose a Schedule You Can Maintain

Post when you have useful news and review old offers when their terms change. A weekly reminder may help you keep up.

  • The original claim that ordinary posts disappear after about seven days does not match Google’s current guide. It says posts older than six months are archived unless a date range is set.
  • One or two posts a week is a suggested routine, not a ranking requirement or guarantee that the profile will stay prominent.
  • A regular time can help with planning, but do not invent news just to fill the schedule.

Tip 5: Explain the Real Place and Service Naturally

Use language a customer understands. Location and service details can be useful, but a list of search phrases does not guarantee broader exposure.

  • Restaurant: describe an actual pasta dish in Gangnam or a real lunch offer in Yeoksam.
  • Café: explain the actual brunch menu or dessert at the Hongdae location.
  • Clinic: provide factual, appropriate information about consultations or booking, without unsupported “best” claims, patient outcomes or restricted product promotion.
Write a readable sentence about the real business. Do not add unrelated places, repeated phrases or claims you cannot support.

Examples for Different Businesses

These are illustrative writing examples adapted from the original article, not current promotions, verified prices or ready-approved medical advertisements. Dates, stock, eligibility and terms must match a real offer before it is used.

Possible action: view the menu or use an available booking button leading to the correct destination.

Example heading: March color-and-cut offer: KRW 89,000
Example description: A color-and-cut package with the scalp-care service described in the offer. The original example limited availability to 20 bookings; use that limit only if it is real, and state what is included and any other conditions.
Possible action: use the supported contact or booking route for details.

Clinic or Korean Medicine Clinic

Example heading: Information about headache consultations
Example description: Contact the clinic to ask about its consultation process and available appointments.
Any examination or further care depends on an individual clinician’s assessment. Keep this an information example, without promising a test, outcome or promotional benefit.
Possible action: an appropriate inquiry or booking destination. The clinic must consider its applicable advertising and privacy requirements before using promotional material.

How to Assess the Response

Begin with the data actually available in your account and the customer action you want to understand.

  • Review applicable Business Profile performance measures, while noting that profile-level activity can include organic and advertising interactions.
  • Do not assume every interface has a universal per-post “views” report. Where a button leads to your website, suitable campaign tagging and analytics can help identify website activity, subject to the actual setup.
  • Compare useful themes and actions over consistent periods. A website click or direction request does not confirm a completed booking, visit or sale.
The original article attributed a 70% search-impression increase after three months of posting to Google data, but did not supply a matching primary study. This edition does not present that percentage as a verified result or promise a posting schedule will produce it.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much can I write?

The original article quoted a 1,500-character maximum and an 80–100-character preview. The current general guide checked for this edition does not establish those as universal limits for every post field or display. Follow the actual editor limit and preview for the selected type and device, and put the useful information near the beginning.

How long does a post remain visible?

Google’s current guide says posts older than six months are archived unless a date range is set. That is different from the original seven-day rule. Display position and reach can change, and a dated offer or event must be accurate for its real period.

Which photo should I use?

Choose a clear, representative image of the real place, product or service. The original 1200 × 900-pixel recommendation is an editing suggestion; check the current upload requirements and cropping for the chosen field. Do not assume a particular size or original photo automatically guarantees more clicks.

Can a post link to my website?

Supported action buttons can direct customers to relevant destinations, such as details, orders or bookings. Available controls vary by type and profile. Check that the destination matches the action and that both the post and destination comply with applicable content rules.

Choose One Useful Update to Begin

Preparing a short post can fit into a small block of time. Five minutes was the original estimate, rather than a guarantee covering photography, offer checks, review or any required professional assessment.

For a first post, choose something customers need to know:

  • A representative dish or product customers can actually order.
  • An accurate offer or event with its current conditions and dates.
  • A useful look at how the business works or a relevant introduction from the owner.

Use posts to keep customers informed, then assess the response. Regular posting alone does not guarantee more exposure.
