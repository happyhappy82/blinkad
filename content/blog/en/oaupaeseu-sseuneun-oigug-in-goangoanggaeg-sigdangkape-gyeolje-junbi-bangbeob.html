If your restaurant or café serves international visitors, you may already have been asked about WOWPASS. It combines travel payment and currency-exchange services with a transportation-card function, making it a useful option for some visitors to Korea.

At the counter, staff need to know how to take the card, explain an unsuccessful payment and offer an available alternative.

In our work on business profiles, we recommend checking the payment setup alongside the signs, staff phrases, profile information and review replies. Together, they help customers understand what to expect at checkout.

What is WOWPASS?

The Korea Tourism Organization describes WOWPASS as a prepaid card for international visitors, combining payment, currency exchange and transportation features. The provider's current guide says the retail balance is used at offline merchants accepting local credit or debit card payments, by inserting the card's IC chip into the reader.

That gives visitors an option for paying at participating restaurants, cafés, convenience stores and shops. The retail WOWPASS balance and the Tmoney transportation balance are separate: tapping to ride a train or bus is different from inserting the card to pay a restaurant bill.

For an owner, start by checking the existing domestic-card terminal and provider setup. Do not assume you need a dedicated WOWPASS terminal, or that enabling international Visa or Mastercard acceptance is the same test.

Why restaurants and cafés should understand it

A payment difficulty can become part of the customer's account of a visit. They may have enjoyed the meal but been confused by a declined card or an unexpected request for cash.

That makes checkout guidance relevant in areas such as Myeongdong, Hongdae, Dongdaemun, Itaewon, Seongsu, Sinchon, Busan and Jeju, wherever the business receives international customers.

A visitor presenting WOWPASS expects to use it as a payment card. Check three things:

  • Does the terminal process the relevant prepaid-card transaction through its domestic-card setup?
  • Do staff understand the difference between WOWPASS, overseas-issued cards and mobile wallets?
  • Can they explain an available alternative if the transaction fails?

1. Test the terminal and payment route

Before advertising a payment option, confirm it with the provider and test the actual route. WOWPASS chip payments, overseas-issued cards, contactless cards, Apple Pay and Samsung Wallet are separate acceptance questions.

Check the following before a customer needs to rely on the answer:

  • Ordinary domestic credit or debit card payments work.
  • You know whether the terminal accepts the relevant overseas-issued Visa or Mastercard cards.
  • You know which contactless services the terminal supports.
  • Staff can interpret the error or decline information available to the merchant.
  • Any genuine payment limitations for a particular order or service are understood and communicated.

A successful international-card test does not establish WOWPASS acceptance, and the reverse is also true. Keep a record of the methods you have actually confirmed.

2. Put clear payment signs where customers can see them

Customers should be able to check their options before ordering. For a business that supports the stated methods, short examples include:

  • Card payments accepted.
  • WOWPASS accepted — please insert the card.
  • Visa / Mastercard accepted.
  • Please pay at the counter.
  • Cash payments also accepted.

Choose only the statements that are accurate for your business. Do not treat a sign as a substitute for confirming the payment setup.

3. Prepare a few staff phrases

A small set of clear phrases helps staff explain the next step:

  • You can pay by card.
  • Please insert your card.
  • Please tap your card here — for a card and reader that support contactless payment.
  • The card payment was declined.
  • Do you have another card?
  • You can also pay in cash.
  • Let me check the payment status before we try again.

For a retail WOWPASS payment, use the insertion instruction in the provider's guide; do not assume the transportation tap function also pays a restaurant bill. If a transaction fails, explain what you can verify and check its status before retrying. This helps avoid unnecessary duplicate attempts.

4. Keep profile information consistent with checkout

Customers researching a visit on Google Maps may consider the menu, photographs, reviews, opening hours and available payment methods.

The payment attributes available to a Business Profile can vary. Use the available fields accurately and show useful, current information in photographs and other permitted content.

  • Update photographs of the actual payment sign.
  • Show the English menu and checkout instructions clearly.
  • Respond when a review raises a relevant payment question.
  • Explain genuine payment limitations before the customer orders.

Keep customer cards, transaction screens and personal details out of public photographs and replies. Make the information easy to find before the visit, rather than relying entirely on a conversation at the till.

5. Review the full range of payment options

WOWPASS is one option. Other visitors may use international cards, Apple Pay, Alipay, WeChat Pay or cash. Familiarity with a brand does not establish whether a particular terminal or QR code accepts it.

Map out the options the business actually supports:

  • Overseas-issued cards.
  • WOWPASS retail payments.
  • Alipay and WeChat Pay, checked separately.
  • Cash.
  • Receipt requests.
  • Payment arrangements for takeaway orders.

The aim is to give a consistent, accurate answer about the available methods. You do not need to display every payment logo a visitor might recognize.

A restaurant and café checklist

  • We have checked international-card acceptance separately from the WOWPASS domestic-card route.
  • Staff know WOWPASS is a visitor prepaid card and retail payment uses chip insertion.
  • We have short English phrases for an unsuccessful payment.
  • The counter sign accurately describes available methods.
  • Our profile has current menu and payment-guidance photographs.
  • We respond appropriately to relevant international-customer reviews.
  • We explain any genuine payment limitations before an order is placed.

Prepare for common checkout questions

Agree on a response to situations such as these, without guessing why a card was declined:

  • An insufficient retail-card balance.
  • A declined overseas-issued card.
  • A terminal that does not support the requested contactless method.
  • A payment limitation that needs explaining before an order.
  • Confusion about paying for takeaway.
  • An unclear process for requesting a receipt.

If a customer needs to check a WOWPASS balance, direct them to their own app or the provider's support. Have a phrase ready for directing the customer to the provider if the issue cannot be resolved at the counter.

Respond to payment experiences in reviews

When a customer comments on checkout, acknowledge the experience naturally. For example:

  • Thank you for visiting. We're glad checkout was straightforward.
  • Thank you for your review. We hope to welcome you again.
  • We're sorry you had difficulty paying. We'll review what happened with our team.

Choose a reply that fits the experience and the follow-up you will actually make. Discuss private transaction details through an appropriate private channel.

Frequently asked questions

Do we need a separate terminal for WOWPASS?

Start by checking the existing domestic-card acceptance route with your provider. WOWPASS's retail guide instructs customers to insert the card's IC chip. Confirm the actual terminal setup before displaying acceptance.

Does accepting WOWPASS resolve every international payment issue?

No. Customers may use overseas-issued cards, mobile wallets or cash. Check the methods your business supports and prepare staff to explain the available options.

Where should we put payment information?

The counter, entrance, relevant menu information and profile photographs are useful places. Customers should be able to check before ordering.

Can a payment difficulty affect a review?

A customer may mention it as part of the visit. Prepare clear explanations and an appropriate response; a smooth payment does not guarantee a favorable review.
